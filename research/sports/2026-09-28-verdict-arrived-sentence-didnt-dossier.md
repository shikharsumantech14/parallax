## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The reader arrives thinking the Manchester City story finally has an ending:
after three and a half years, a panel said *guilty*, and now comes the
punishment. The structural truth is that "guilty" and "punished" are two
different proceedings in English football, and only the first one has
happened — there is no published decision, no sanction, and an appeal route
still ahead. The mechanism that creates the gap is this: the Premier League
does not have a court. It has **arbitration** — a private process designed
for commercial disputes between roughly equal parties who both want a quick,
confidential answer. Arbitration's defining features are that it has no
external clock, that its confidentiality is the default and publication the
exception, and that its speed is a function of how hard the parties fight.
Those features are neutral between equals. Put them in front of a member
club with the deepest litigation capacity in the competition and they stop
being neutral: the cases that resolved fast (Nottingham Forest, 63 days;
Everton, a five-day hearing; Chelsea, a negotiated sanction agreement) are
precisely the cases where the club **admitted the breach and cooperated** —
each of those clubs was explicitly credited with cooperation as mitigation.
The cases that fight take years, and the years themselves are the outcome,
because the competition kept running: titles were won, records were set,
transfer windows opened and closed, and every one of those results is now
final in a way the verdict against them is not. The punchline the reader
doesn't see coming: the legal principle that sports arbitral decisions
*should* be published — the one the Premier League's own Rule W.82.2
encodes and that a Harvard law review cites as the leading authority — was
established in the English Court of Appeal in a case brought by Manchester
City itself.

## 2. Why now

- **25 September 2026** — multiple outlets report that an independent
  Commission has found Manchester City guilty on **114 of 115** Premier
  League charges. **[UNVERIFIED — see §9]** The Premier League has, as of
  28 September 2026, published **no statement and no final award** on the
  Commission's decision; a domain-restricted search of premierleague.com on
  2026-09-28 surfaced its statements on Everton, Nottingham Forest, Chelsea
  and Leicester City, but no Manchester City decision statement.
- The absence is itself the news, because the league's own rule requires
  publication: *"Under Premier League Rule W.82.2, the Commission's final
  award will be published on the Premier League's website."*
  ([Premier League, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3045970))
- **Elapsed, charge to reported verdict:** 2023-02-06 → 2026-09-25 =
  1,096 days (three full years) + 231 days = **1,327 days**;
  1,327 ÷ 365.25 = **3.63 years**.
- **Days since the reported verdict with no published award:** 2026-09-25 →
  2026-09-28 = **3 days** (and counting at draft time).

### 4a-bis. The appeal stage, anchored (added by the top-up, 2026-09-28)

The check pass flagged that both appeal outcomes in §4e were cited to Premier
League statements that predate the appeals. One is now anchored; one is not.

- **Nottingham Forest — ANCHORED.** An independent Appeal Board dismissed the
  club's appeal on **7 May 2024**: *"The four-point deduction will therefore
  remain in place."* The club appealed on **two grounds** — that the Commission
  erred in not treating the sale of a high-profile player shortly after the
  assessment period as a mitigating factor, and that it erred in electing not
  to suspend some or all of the deduction. **Both grounds were rejected**; the
  Appeal Board found the Commission was entitled to impose the sanction it did
  — [PL, 7 May 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3999776)
  - **Appeal Board members:** the Rt Hon **Lord Dyson** (chair),
    **David Phillips KC**, **Daniel Alexander KC** — same source.
  - **The structural point:** Forest's appeal took **50 days** and the panel
    that heard it was named and dated in a public statement on the day it
    decided. City's first-instance decision has been reported for 3 days with
    no statement at all.
- **Everton — STILL NOT ANCHORED.** The 19 Apr 2024 Premier League "Appeal
  Board update" *"No further comment will be made in respect of the case until
  the Appeal Board's decision is issued"* concerns the appeal against the
  **two-point** deduction, not the 10 → 6 reduction of February 2024. It
  records the Board's intention to announce *"before the last day of the

  [PL, 19 Apr 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3972848).
  The February 2024 "reduced to 6" statement was not reachable within this

### 4i. Indian ground (contract §3 rule 4)

- **Currency conversion (CURRENT figures only).** Rate: **£1 = ₹126.9378** on

  - Chelsea's total fine, in force March 2026: £10m + £0.75m = **£10.75m**;
    £10.75m × 126.9378 = ₹1,364,581,350 = **₹136.46 crore**

  - The PSR loss limit still in force: £105m × 126.9378 = ₹13,328,469,000 =
    **₹1,332.85 crore** (13,328,469,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = 1,332.85).
  - **Do NOT convert** the 2023/2024 Everton and Forest figures (£124.5m,
    £19.5m, £16.6m, £34.5m, £61m) — historical, stay unconverted.
- **Indian institutional anchor — the Lodha Committee.** India ran the same
  experiment in cricket and reached the opposite conclusion: that a sport's
  own members cannot be trusted to judge themselves, and that the remedy is
  standing independent officers rather than ad-hoc panels.
  - The committee was constituted *"as per the judgment and order of the
    Hon'ble Supreme Court dated 22nd January 2015"* and comprised
    **Justice R.M. Lodha, former Chief Justice of India, Justice Ashok Bhan
    and Justice R.V. Raveendran**, both former Supreme Court judges —
    [Lodha Committee Report](https://gujaratcricketassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Lodha_Committee_Report.pdf)
  - Its recommendation, verbatim: *"There also ought to be an Ombudsman, an
    Ethics Officer and an Electoral Officer who can provide institutional
    resolution while principles of transparency and conflict of interest need
    to be infused without further delay."* — same source
  - The contrast to draw: the Premier League's Commission is **convened per
    case and dissolved after**; Lodha's design is **standing office-holders
    who exist before the dispute does**. A standing officer has a calendar; a
    per-case panel does not.
- **Second Indian anchor — a live Indian football dispute, decided in nine
