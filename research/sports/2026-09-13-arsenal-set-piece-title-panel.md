## The hook

> "Arsenal scored 19 league goals from corners, a Premier League record, and
> conceded 27 all season. You were watching a factory, not a football team."

- **Aarav — yes.** Two numbers, one of them ranked ("a record"), and "factory"
  is a thing he can picture. One snag: *"You were watching"* assumes he watched
  the Premier League last season. He did not, and the sentence quietly tells him
  he missed something he was never at. The twist survives it.
- **Meera — yes.** The record and the 27 are enough; she reads on because the
  title already told her the finding and the hook told her the shape of the
  proof.
- **Sana — yes, and this is the one that stopped the thumb.** "Factory, not a
  football team" is the line she would send to someone. The title's "corner
  flag" gives her the thing to picture before the hook even lands.
- **Karthik — yes.** No Hindi in the hook; it does its whole job in English.

The title states the finding, the primer is three short sentences and does its
three jobs, and the first graphic arrives immediately. The head is the strongest
block in the issue.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 7: the closing note is the issue's one unretellable block.** ".3"
   and ".75 to .80" arrive with no owner, no unit the reader counts in and
   nothing to measure against — and they sit directly under "Arsenal
   manufactured 19 of them at a corner", so all four readers tried to read them
   as *Arsenal's* rate. That is the join storyboard §5C explicitly forbids.
   Either give the rates a stated owner and something felt to sit beside, or cut
   them and let the money finding stand alone. ⚠ verify: as placed, the note
   invites the reader to slot Arsenal into a band the source does not put them
   in.
2. **Section 8: "sit deep" and "hold the line high" contradict each other** for
   anyone who does not already know the shape. The sentence has to say what the
   high line is high *relative to*, in the same sentence — three of four readers
   re-read it and one came away with the shape only.
3. **Section 6, the hero: three new terms in the opening clause.** "Rehearsed
   delivery", "blocking screens" and "near-post runs" all arrive before any of
   them is explained, and *delivery* is a cricket false friend on an Indian
   sports desk. The hero's first sentence should carry at most one new term; the
   `plain` line and caption are doing the rescuing that the intro should not
   need.
4. **Section 1 leaks the vocabulary one section before the glossary catches
   it.** "Restarts" and "dropped deeper" both land in the `you-think` panel;
   `jargon-buster` is the next section. Either the opening panel uses the plain
   words, or the glossary moves ahead of it.
5. **"Block" is a term of art used three times and never explained** — as a noun
   in §3's intro, as §8's eyebrow, and in §9's title, where it carries the
   finding. It is the one word in the issue doing load-bearing work with no
   gloss anywhere.
6. **Section 3, tile 2: 28.3 has a rank but nothing to feel.** Tile 1 does this
   perfectly — "eight fewer than any other side. Manchester City next, on 35."
   Tile 2's "lowest in the league… best in Europe's top five leagues too" tells
   the reader where it sits without telling them how big it is. Also: the tile
   writes out "expected goals **against**", and the glossary glossed "xG", not
   xG-against; the reader has to invert it themselves.
7. **Section 4: "2nd team / 3rd team / 4th team" reads as reserve sides.** In
   Indian football usage a club's "2nd team" is its reserves. The labels need to
   say these are the next-ranked clubs on the same measure.
8. **Section 9 uses "penalty" for two different things in one card.** The intro
   says Arsenal "lost… on penalties" (the shootout), the xG row says the only
   goal conceded "was a penalty" (the kick), and the scoreline shows 1–1 with
   PSG marked the winner and no shootout score anywhere. Every reader worked it
   out; three of four worked it out twice.
9. **Section 3, tile 3 counts throw-ins as set pieces** ("19 from corners. Four
   from free-kicks, two from throw-ins"), while §2's gloss defines a set piece
   as "a corner or a free kick". The arithmetic works; the category does not
   match its own definition two sections earlier.
10. **Karthik: *maidan* is the subject of the closer's one image.** It passes
    the skip test only because the clause after it repeats the picture. If any
    Hindi word in this issue is moved off a noun slot and onto something beside
    a noun, it is this one.
