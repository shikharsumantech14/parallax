## 1. The argument in one line

Finding a club guilty and punishing it are two separate proceedings, and 1,330
days after the charge only the first has happened, because no rule puts a clock
on the second.

## 2. The hero

**`power-matrix`** (G6 · composition of control), **row 8**, `layout: wide`.

It renders dossier §4f whole: eight stages of the Premier League's
disciplinary pipeline crossed with four parties (the League Board, the
independent Judicial Panel, the charged club, the other 19 clubs), each cell
`full` / `partial` / `none` / `contested`. The bottom row, **Setting the
deadline**, is `none` all the way across, and that empty row is the argument
the whole issue has been walking toward. Rule W.82 makes the hearing
confidential and Rule W.82.2 requires the final award to be published; neither,
as quoted in the League's own statement, attaches a time limit (§4f, §4a).

Why it and not the ranked charts: the story is not how big the numbers are, it
is who can make the clock move. Nobody can. An empty row is the strongest mark
this kind has, and no other kind in the catalog draws an absence of control.

`wide`, never `split` or `bleed` (aliases of `wide` on the floor plan since
2026-09-23): eight rows by four columns needs the full column to stay legible.

## 6. The three questions

1. **Q:** A panel has reportedly found against Manchester City. What still has not happened? · **A:** No final award has been published and no sanction exists. The League's own Rule W.82.2 says the final award will be published on its website, and 1,330 days after the charge nothing has appeared. · dossier §4a (Rule W.82.2), §4c (0 published, 0 sanctioned, verified by absence 2026-09-28)
2. **Q:** Why did Nottingham Forest's whole case, appeal included, take 113 days while City's first stage has taken 1,330? · **A:** Forest admitted the breach and was credited with exceptional cooperation; arbitration's speed depends on how hard the parties fight, and no rule sets a deadline. · dossier §4e (63 + 50 = 113), §4h (cooperation credited for Forest and Chelsea), §4f (no deadline row)
3. **Q:** Who could force the decision to be published, or set a date for it? · **A:** Nobody in the pipeline. The Board brings the charge, the independent Judicial Panel appoints the Commission and sets the sanction, the club can appeal and challenge further, and not one party holds the clock. India's Lodha Committee answered the same problem by asking for standing officers instead of a panel convened per case. · dossier §4f (bottom row `none` × 4), §4i (Lodha)

## 7. Names

Ten, inside the cap of twelve.

1. **Manchester City** — the champion club charged in February 2023 and reported, not confirmed, to have lost.
2. **the Premier League** — the competition that brought the charge and writes the rules the case is heard under.
3. **the independent Commission** — the panel that hears a charged club, appointed by the League's independent Judicial Panel, not by the League itself.
4. **the Appeal Board** — the second panel a club can take a decision to, as Forest did.
5. **Everton** — the club that admitted a £124.5m loss against a £105m limit and lost ten points for it.
6. **Nottingham Forest** — the club that admitted its breach and had the whole thing over, appeal included, in 113 days.
7. **Chelsea** — the club whose new owners reported themselves and settled the punishment by agreement.
8. **Lord Dyson** — a judge on the appeal panel that took Forest's case (use this phrase; "former Master of the Rolls" is [UNVERIFIED], §4b).
9. **Justice R.M. Lodha** — a former Chief Justice of India, who chaired the Supreme Court committee that rewrote how Indian cricket governs itself.
10. **Anwar Ali** — the India defender whose contract case moved from suspension to clearance in nine days.

## 8. Composer notes

**How each block and flag in the check report was worked around.**

1. **BLOCKED driver — the verdict itself has no allowlisted source (§7.1).** No
   graphic is built on it. Every row above renders either a T0 Premier League
   statement or the verified absence of one. The reported verdict appears twice
   and only twice, both times as something *you read*: in the hook ("You read
   that City were found guilty") and on the `you-think` card's left panel.
   Parallax never says guilty in its own voice, never states or implies what
   sanction is coming, and never paraphrases a press report into quotation
   marks (§9.9).
2. **BLOCKED driver — 115 versus 130 (§7.2).** The `bill-funnel` the dossier
   captured in §4c is **cut**. It cannot be rebuilt on the League's five
   categories either, because no source gives how many categories were upheld,
   and a funnel needs a count at every stage. Neither 115 nor 114 nor 130 may
   be printed as a figure anywhere in the issue. Where the drafter needs the
   scope of the charge, it is "five categories of alleged breach, covering
   2009/10 to 2022/23" (§4a) — the League's own framing, T0. The attrition idea
   the funnel carried survives in row 1's two zeros and row 6's empty bar.
   `bill-funnel` was in any case already claimed as new by the 2026-09-21
   politics storyboard, so cutting it costs no novelty.
3. **Everton's "reduced to 6" is unanchored (§3 row 2, §3 row 6).** Row 6's bar
   for Everton is **10**, the anchored figure. The numbers 6, 8 and 0.31 pts/£m
   are forbidden in the draft. The caption may say an appeal later reduced the
   deduction, without a figure, and must not imply the 10 stood.
4. **"Everton 8 months" has no stated inputs (§2 row 23).** Dropped. The
   timeline's Everton event is the 17 November 2023 decision only.
5. **The 2017/18 superlative rests on Opta alone (§2 row 12).** The timeline
   event states 100 points and 19 points clear, sourced to Opta Analyst. It
   does **not** say "the largest margin in Premier League history".
6. **The £→₹ rate has no T0–T2 anchor (§7.4).** Both conversions are hedged to
   "about ₹136 crore" and "about ₹1,330 crore", and each section source line
   carries the rate and its date (£1 = ₹126.94, 27 September 2026).
7. **"The deepest litigation capacity in the competition" is unsourced (§2 row
   19).** Never asserted. The same point is made with an anchored fact instead:
   the publication principle the League's rule encodes was settled in 2021 in a
   Court of Appeal case *Manchester City itself brought*, and lost. That lands
   in the hero's caption, where it also explains the one `contested` cell.
8. **Everton was not credited with cooperation (§2 row 20).** Copy says Everton
   *admitted* the breach. "Exceptional cooperation" is said only of Chelsea and
   Nottingham Forest, which is what §4h anchors.
9. **The Sep–Dec 2024 hearing window is [UNVERIFIED] with no resolution path.**
   It appears in no row. The `latency-waterfall` therefore draws only referral
   → decision → appeal, never a hearing span.
10. **2013/14's margin** is not used anywhere.
11. **Publisher-spread block (§7.3).** Not fixable from the storyboard, and the
    operator must rule on the denominator. Mitigation inside the issue: the PSR
    gloss in row 5 carries one UEFA line (aggregate pre-tax losses of €1.2bn
    across European top-division clubs, about 4% of revenue — §4a-ter), so a
    second T0 publisher is visible to the reader. It is attributed to UEFA and
    **never** to the Premier League specifically, per §4a-ter's caveat.

**Kinds considered and rejected.**

- **`elo-river`**. Ruled out by the dossier itself (§7): no named-model rating
  series covers the charge window, and season points are not a rating.
- **`number-sense`** on CAS publishing 43 of about 600 awards in 2019 (§4g,
  7.17%). A clean, anchored card and a good one. Cut for the nine-beat ceiling
  and the three-card cap, not for want of data. If the operator wants ten rows,
  this is the row to add, after row 5.
- **`quote`** on Rule W.82.2. Folded into the hero's intro, verbatim and
  attributed, so the issue keeps nine rows. The researcher is right that this
  rule is the strongest voice available (§9.7); it does not need its own
  section to be heard. Add it back as a quiet row 9 if the operator prefers.
- **`paradox`**. Tempting for "confidential by default / must be published",
  but they are one rule and its sub-rule, not two facts pulling apart. The
  hero's grid says it better.
- **`three-steps`** for the cooperation lever. Dropped to stay inside three
  plain-language cards and to keep the legal hedging in prose-shaped intros,
  where attribution is easier. The lever lands in row 6's intro.

**Authoring notes for the drafter.**

- `margin-ladder` takes a numeric margin per rung, so **only three rungs are
  drawn**: Forest £34.5m, Everton £19.5m, Everton £16.6m. Manchester City is
  named in the intro, the caption and the plain line as the case with no figure
  to place, because no award has been published. Do **not** author City at
  margin 0 — a zero-length rung reads as zero overspend, which is a claim the
  dossier does not make and the League has never published.
- `latency-waterfall` is authored on a days-since-referral clock, unit `days`:
  Forest stage one start 0 / duration 63; Forest appeal start 63 / duration 50;
  Manchester City start 0 / duration 1,330, and the caption must say that last
  bar has not stopped. Use **1,330** (2023-02-06 → 2026-09-28, both anchored),
  never 1,327, which depends on the unverified verdict date.
- Restate after every graphic (contract §3 rule 5): the next section's intro
  says in words what the figure just showed.
- Modes: row 3 and row 8 CALM-STRUCTURAL, rows 2, 4, 5 and 9 CONVERSATIONAL
  EXPLAINER, rows 6 and 7 FORENSIC. No SATIRICAL section: this is live,
  unresolved disciplinary litigation.
