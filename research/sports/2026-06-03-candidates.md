## C-01 · The Club That Stopped Buying the Trophy

- **status:** open
- **why now:** On May 30, 2026 PSG beat Arsenal on penalties in Budapest to
  retain the Champions League — the first club to win it back-to-back since
  Real Madrid in 2016/17. They did it the season *after* the Mbappé–Neymar–
  Messi galácticos era ended, fielding four academy graduates in Europe and
  publicly enforcing a wage ceiling (they sold Donnarumma to Manchester City
  in Sept 2025 rather than meet his renewal). In the same window the Premier
  League voted (14–6, the bare two-thirds) to replace PSR with a Squad Cost
  Ratio capping squad spend at 70% of revenue for European clubs from 2026/27.
- **angle:** The conventional frame is "petro-club finally wins, then wins
  again." The Parallax frame is the inversion: PSG won back-to-back European
  titles only *after* abandoning the strategy that defined them. The issue
  traces a 14-year arc — from a 2011 takeover and €580m of post-2021 losses
  buying superstars who never delivered the Cup, to a Luis Enrique side built
  on a wage ratio and academy minutes — and lands it against the exact moment
  the rules of the game change: the death of PSR and the arrival of the Squad
  Cost Ratio. The structural revelation: the financial model that "bought the
  Champions League" was retired *before* it won the Champions League, and the
  whole league is now being forced toward the discipline PSG stumbled into.

## C-04 · The Celebration That Killed Eleven People

- **status:** open
- **why now:** RCB's 2026 title (May 31) produced *no* victory parade in
  Bengaluru — a direct consequence of June 4, 2025, when a crowd crush outside
  the M. Chinnaswamy Stadium during the first-title felicitation killed 11
  people and injured more than 50. A Central Administrative Tribunal found RCB
  "responsible," ruling the franchise drew three-to-five lakh people via a
  sudden social-media announcement without police permission; the Karnataka
  government's report held RCB, event partner DNA Networks and the KSCA
  jointly accountable. One year on, the second title was marked in silence.
- **angle:** The conventional frame is "tragic stampede, lessons learned." The
  Parallax frame is structural and uncomfortable: modern sport manufactures
  mass-gathering events — the open-top parade, the stadium felicitation — as a
  product, while the safety architecture (permissions, crowd modelling,
  gate-flow, who is legally liable) lags decades behind the spectacle. The
  issue rebuilds the 90 minutes from the 3:14pm free-pass tweet to the crush at
  eight gates, maps the chain of responsibility the tribunal traced, and asks a
  comparative question: why do football and cricket keep killing fans at
  celebrations (Hillsborough → Kanjuruhan → Chinnaswamy) when the failure mode
  is always the same — capacity announced faster than it can be managed? The
  structural revelation: the parade is an unregulated venue, and the 2026
  silence is the only safety system that actually worked.
