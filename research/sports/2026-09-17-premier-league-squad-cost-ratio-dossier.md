## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

A reader meets a spending cap and hears a ceiling: a line above which nobody
may go, the same line for everybody. The Premier League's new Squad Cost
Ratio is not that. It caps squad spending at **85% of the club's own football
revenue**, which means the line is drawn in a different place for each of the
twenty clubs, and it is drawn highest for the clubs that already earn most.
The old rule, PSR, capped a **loss in pounds** — £105m over three years, the
same number for Bournemouth and for Manchester City. The new rule caps a
**share of yourself**. Changing the denominator from a fixed sum to each
club's own revenue converts a restraint into a multiplier: Manchester City's
85% is £590m of legal squad spending, Bournemouth's 85% is £155m, and the
gap between those two ceilings is not a loophole in the rule, it is the rule.
The issue rebuilds this in three moves: where a club's revenue actually comes
from (and why broadcast money, the one stream that is shared nearly equally,
is 82% of Bournemouth's income and 40% of Manchester City's); what the ratio
therefore permits at each end of the table; and the thing the accounts say
out loud — that the £948m of losses the rule is meant to answer did not come
from wages, which barely moved, but from trading players and assets, which is
the exact activity the new numerator counts. A percentage cap is a ranking
system wearing a rulebook, and it was voted in by the clubs it ranks.

## 2. Why now

**The 2026-27 season, five gameweeks old as of 2026-09-17, is the first in
which Squad Cost Ratio (SCR) and Sustainability and Systemic Resilience (SSR)
actually bind.** Premier League clubs approved both at a shareholders' meeting
on **21 November 2025**, and PSR no longer applies from 2026/27
([Premier League statement](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4468470/premier-league-statement-new-financial-system)).

The backdrop landed seven weeks before the season: **Deloitte's Annual Review
of Football Finance 2026, published 8 July 2026**, covering 2024/25. Premier
League clubs' aggregate pre-tax losses went from **£135m to £948m** in one
season — a sevenfold rise — while the wages-to-revenue ratio barely moved
(64% → 65%) and revenue rose 8% to a record £6.8bn. Clubs in operating profit
fell from thirteen to eight
([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/european-football-revenues-surpass-40bn-for-the-first-time.html)).

## 4. Key facts & data

### 4.1 The rule itself — all T0, `premierleague.com`

- **SCR green threshold:** squad costs capped at **85% of football revenue and
  net profit/loss from player sales** — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **What counts as squad costs:** player and head-coach wages, agents' fees,
  amortisation or impairment of transfer fees — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **Multi-year allowance:** 30%, for all clubs including promoted clubs. Falls
  by the size of any breach of 85% at the Accounts Confirmation Test; can be
  rebuilt 10% a year (the "Positive Feedback Loop") back up to 30% — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **Red threshold:** **115%** (reduced by the Feedback Loop where a club has
  breached) — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **Between 85% and 115%:** a financial **levy**, no sporting sanction.
  Levy = the smaller of the in-season or post-season overspend × the
  percentage over 85%. The League's own worked example: **a £250,000 overspend
  at a 89% ratio = a £10,000 fine** — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **Above 115%:** **6-point deduction plus 1 further point per £6.5m** spent
  over the threshold — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **Test calendar:** Compliance Test **1 March**; Accounts Confirmation Test
  **June**; True-Up **October** — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **SSR positive equity test:** ≤ **90%** in 2026/27, ≤ **85%** in 2027/28,
  ≤ **80%** from 2028/29. Assessed **7 July** annually. SSR's three tests are
  Working Capital, Liquidity and Positive Equity — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4468470/premier-league-statement-new-financial-system)
- **Levies first payable:** from 2027/28 — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained)
- **The voting threshold:** "All rule changes and major commercial contracts
  require the support of at least a two-thirds vote, or **14 clubs**, to be
  agreed." Each member club has **one vote** — [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/about)
- **What PSR was:** a profit-and-loss constraint permitting maximum losses of
  **£105m over three years** — [The Swiss Ramble](https://swissramble.substack.com/p/psr-is-dead-long-live-scr)
- **RESOLVED — the 70% vs 85% conflict flagged in the candidate.** Both are
  correct and belong to different regimes. **Premier League:** green 85%, red
  115%. **Clubs in UEFA competition:** green **70%**, red **100%** — UEFA's own
  squad cost rule, which a qualifying English club must meet *as well* —
  [The Swiss Ramble](https://swissramble.substack.com/p/psr-is-dead-long-live-scr).
  The drafter must say which regime each number belongs to every time.

### 4.8 Where the losses came from — the claim the issue turns on

- Deloitte, on the £812m increase: "much of the £812m increase in pre-tax
  losses was attributable to **transaction decisions involving either players
  or other club assets**" — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/services/consulting/research/annual-review-of-football-finance-premier-league-clubs.html)
- **Wages did not cause it:** wages/revenue moved 64% → 65% in the same season
  the losses rose sevenfold — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/european-football-revenues-surpass-40bn-for-the-first-time.html)
- **A worked instance.** Aston Villa's reported £17m pre-tax **profit**
  depended on **£114m of gains from selling the women's team and property
  rights to group companies**; excluding those asset sales the pre-tax result
  would have been a **£97m loss**. Villa's operating loss was £140m —
  [Swiss Ramble](https://swissramble.substack.com/p/aston-villa-finances-202425)
- **Manchester City** swung from a £74m pre-tax profit to a **£10m loss**,
  driven chiefly by a £44m reduction in player-sale profits — [Swiss Ramble](https://swissramble.substack.com/p/manchester-city-finances-202425)
- **The transfer market is where English money goes.** Over 2015–2024 the
  Premier League invested **€23.02bn**, **28.1%** of worldwide transfer
  spending; Serie A was second at €10.84bn. Chelsea led the world at €2.78bn
  invested; Manchester United had the worst net balance anywhere at
  **−€1.304bn**. Europe's big five were about two-thirds of global spending —
  [CIES Football Observatory, Monthly Report 97](https://football-observatory.com/IMG/sites/mr/mr97/en/)
  (Poli, Ravenel & Besson)

### 9.4 The one thing that could sink the issue, and the guard against it

**§4.6's `y` values are Parallax arithmetic, not filed ratios.** No club's
official Squad Cost Ratio is public, and it cannot be reconstructed from
published accounts because agents' fees are not separately disclosed and net
player-sale profit is not in the revenue line. The dossier's calculation
(wages + player amortisation ÷ revenue) uses only sourced components and is
directionally sound, but it **overstates every club's real ratio**, and it
overstates it most at the clubs that sell the most players. If the draft ever
calls these numbers "the Squad Cost Ratio", the issue is making a false claim
about a regulatory measurement. The axis label, the caption and the `plain`
line must all say what the number is. **The verifier should treat any
appearance of the phrase "squad cost ratio" next to these seven values as a
blocking flag.**

The same discipline applies to §4.5: wages-to-revenue is **not** the SCR
numerator, so the 85% line does not belong on that chart.
