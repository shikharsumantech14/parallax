## C-04 · The El Niño That Could Lock In a New Floor

- **status:** chosen
- **why now:** Multiple climate agencies — NOAA, China's National Climate Center, Carbon Brief's quarterly analysis published in late April 2026 — are converging on a forecast of a strong or "super" El Niño developing by autumn 2026. Climate models give a median estimate of 2.2°C warming in the Niño3.4 region by September. If that materialises, 2026 could become the second-warmest year on record, continuing the streak where 2023, 2024, and 2025 are the three warmest years ever.
- **angle:** The structural story is not the weather event — it is the ratchet mechanism. Each El Niño since 2015 has set a new temperature floor that the subsequent La Niña fails to fully undo, leaving the baseline warmer after every cycle. Parallax shows the step-function: not a smooth upward line but a staircase, where warm years jolt the system to a new level and cool years no longer return to the old one. The WMO State of Climate 2025 report (March 2026) adds the first-ever Earth energy imbalance metric — 91% of excess heat absorbed by oceans — which explains why the floor keeps rising.
