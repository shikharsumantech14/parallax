## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

Readers understand El Niño as a temporary Pacific weather oscillation: warm years spike
upward, La Niña years return temperatures to the long-run average, and the system
oscillates. What the data actually shows since 2015 is a staircase, not a sine wave. The
2022 triple-La-Niña year — three consecutive years of cooling Pacific conditions — was
still the 5th or 6th warmest in 176 years of records. The 2025 La Niña year landed at
1.44°C above pre-industrial, effectively tied with 2023 as second-warmest ever; without
ENSO effects, 2025 would have been the all-time warmest year on record. The mechanism
that explains the ratchet is Earth's energy imbalance: 91% of excess greenhouse heat is
absorbed by the ocean, which never releases it back to space. Each El Niño transfers
ocean-stored heat to the atmosphere and temporarily raises surface temperatures; when
La Niña suppresses Pacific sea-surface temperatures again, it cannot return the global
mean to its pre-El Niño level because the ocean reservoir is permanently warmer than
before. The forecasted 2026 super El Niño — models converge on a median Niño3.4
anomaly of +2.2°C by September, putting it firmly in "super" territory — will set a new
peak; the subsequent La Niña will once again fail to drain the reservoir, establishing a
new, higher floor. What the public reads as a spike is actually each step on a staircase
where no downward riser ever reaches the previous landing.

## 2. Why now

**The concrete anchor (April 2026):** Multiple converging events in the last two weeks of
April 2026 have locked the 2026 El Niño forecast into high confidence:

- **April 9, 2026** — NOAA's Climate Prediction Center issued a formal El Niño Watch,
  citing five consecutive months of subsurface Pacific warming and the emergence of
  westerly wind anomalies over the western equatorial Pacific.
- **April 24, 2026** — WMO announced "high confidence in the onset of El Niño, followed
  by further intensification" (Wilfran Moufouma Okia, Chief of Climate Prediction, WMO).
  Probability of El Niño by May–July: 61%.
- **Late April 2026** — Carbon Brief's Q1 2026 State of the Climate analysis (published
  within the 60-day window) confirmed that Q1 2026 was the fourth-warmest start to any
  year on record, *despite* lingering weak La Niña suppression — and projected 2026 at
  62% probability of being second-warmest year (best estimate: 1.47°C above
  pre-industrial).
- **March 23, 2026** — WMO released the State of Global Climate 2025 report, which for
  the first time ever included Earth's energy imbalance as a key climate indicator, and
  reported it had reached a new high in 2025. This is the institutional framing that
  makes the staircase mechanism legible to a non-specialist audience.

## 4. Key facts & data

### Temperature staircase (the ratchet in numbers)

- **2022 La Niña year (post-2015–16 El Niño floor):** 1.1–1.3°C above pre-industrial; ranked 5th–6th warmest in 176 years. Without La Niña effects would have ranked 2nd warmest. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-how-the-world-warmed-in-2022/)
- **2023 (El Niño developing):** 1.34–1.54°C above pre-industrial; new record; beat 2016 by 0.14–0.17°C; El Niño contribution only +0.013°C. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-2023-smashes-records-for-surface-temperature-and-ocean-heat/)
- **2024 (El Niño peak):** 1.55°C above pre-industrial (WMO synthesis); first year above 1.5°C in most datasets; El Niño contribution +0.128°C; land temperatures 2.3°C above pre-industrial (record). — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-2024-sets-a-new-record-as-the-first-year-above-1-5c/)
- **2025 (La Niña year — post-2023–24 El Niño floor):** 1.44°C above pre-industrial (average of 8 datasets); effectively tied with 2023 as 2nd warmest ever; La Niña contributed −0.05°C. **Without ENSO effects, 2025 would have been the warmest year in the observational record.** — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-2025-in-top-three-hottest-years-on-record-as-ocean-heat-surges/)
- **Floor comparison:** Post-2015–16 El Niño La Niña floor: ~1.1–1.3°C. Post-2023–24 El Niño La Niña floor: ~1.44°C. Net floor rise across one decade: ~0.14–0.34°C. — [Carbon Brief, multiple articles]

### 2026 El Niño forecast

- **El Niño probability:** 61% chance El Niño develops by May–July 2026; expected to persist through end of 2026. — [NOAA CPC](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml)
- **Niño3.4 median forecast:** +2.2°C warming by September 2026 — super El Niño territory (threshold: +2.0°C). — [Carbon Brief, 637 model runs from 13 modeling groups](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Super El Niño definition:** Central Pacific SST +2–3°C above historical averages; comparable to the 2015 "Godzilla El Niño." — [WMO](https://wmo.int/media/news/2026-super-el-nino-how-are-we-preparing-its-impact-latin-america-and-caribbean)
- **ENSO strength probabilities:** 1-in-4 chance Niño3.4 reaches +2.0°C or higher; 1-in-3 chance of "strong" El Niño (≥+1.5°C) by Oct–Dec 2026. — [NOAA CPC](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml)

### 2026 temperature projection

- **Best estimate:** 1.47°C above pre-industrial (range: 1.37–1.58°C across 5 datasets). — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Probability second-warmest year:** 62%. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Probability warmest year ever:** 19%. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Probability exceeds 1.5°C:** ~30%. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Q1 2026 ranking:** Fourth-warmest start to any year on record, despite weak La Niña suppression. 5.2% of Earth's surface experienced record warmth in Q1. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Lag effect:** If strong El Niño peaks in late 2026, 2027 becomes the likely warmest year on record (historical ~3-month lag between Pacific SST peak and global temperature response, as seen in 1998, 2016, and 2024). — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)
- **Potential 2027 impact:** A moderately strong El Niño could drive global average temperature to ~1.7°C above pre-industrial. — [ENSO forecast summary via search results from wmo.int/noaa.gov]

### Earth's energy imbalance and ocean heat

- **91% figure:** More than 91% of excess heat from greenhouse gas imbalance is stored in the ocean. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance), March 23, 2026
- **5% land, 3% ice, 1% atmosphere:** Breakdown of the remaining excess heat. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance)
- **First-ever WMO indicator:** Earth's energy imbalance included for the first time as a key climate indicator in WMO SOGC 2025 report. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2025), published March 23, 2026
- **Imbalance trend:** Earth's energy imbalance has increased since 1960 and reached a new high in 2025. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance)
- **Ocean warming rate:** Ocean warming rate more than doubled from the 1960–2005 period to the 2005–2025 period. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2025)
- **Ocean heat content record:** 2025 set a new record for ocean heat content: approximately 23 zettajoules of heat added — equivalent to 39 times total annual human energy production. Largest year-over-year increase since 2017. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-2025-in-top-three-hottest-years-on-record-as-ocean-heat-surges/)
- **2024 ocean heat:** In 2024, ocean heat increase in a single year equalled ~25 times all human energy production globally in 2023. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-2024-sets-a-new-record-as-the-first-year-above-1-5c/)
- **Ocean surface heatwaves:** ~90% of ocean surface area experienced at least one marine heatwave in 2025. — [WMO, via search summary](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2025)
- **Historical ocean heat:** Even in 2018, the ocean set a new heat content record; upwards of 90% of trapped heat has been going into oceans for decades. — [Carbon Brief 2018](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-new-record-ocean-heat-content-and-growing-a-el-nino/), published Oct 23, 2018

### Greenhouse gases

- **CO₂ in 2024:** Highest in 2 million years. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance)
- **Methane and nitrous oxide in 2024:** Highest in at least 800,000 years. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance)
- **2024 CO₂ increase:** Largest annual increase since measurements began in 1957. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance)

### Causes of recent anomalous warmth (structural context)

- **Long-term trend acceleration:** 2023 was 0.18°C warmer than the 1970–2014 linear trend predicted; 2024 was 0.25°C warmer; 2025 was ~0.11°C warmer. — [Carbon Brief causes analysis](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-what-are-the-causes-of-recent-record-high-global-temperatures/)
- **Shipping sulfur:** IMO 2020 regulations cut shipping fuel sulfur by 80%; contributed approximately +0.04–0.05°C of warming 2020–2024 (reduced SO₂ aerosol cooling). — [Carbon Brief causes analysis](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-what-are-the-causes-of-recent-record-high-global-temperatures/)
- **Solar cycle:** Contributed ~+0.07°C to 2024 temperatures. — [Carbon Brief causes analysis](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-what-are-the-causes-of-recent-record-high-global-temperatures/)
- **Planetary reflectivity decline:** "A significant decline in planetary reflectivity...unprecedented in the satellite record" — raises question of whether recent warmth is partly a permanent shift. — [Carbon Brief causes analysis](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-what-are-the-causes-of-recent-record-high-global-temperatures/)

### Arctic sea ice

- **Winter 2026 maximum:** 14.29 million km² on March 15, 2026 — joint-smallest record with 2025 in nearly 50 years of satellite data. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/state-of-the-climate-strong-el-nino-puts-2026-on-track-for-second-warmest-year/)

### 11 hottest years

- **2015–2025**: Confirmed as the eleven hottest years in 176 years of instrumental records, in order. — [WMO](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2025)

**Data visualisation notes:**
- The `climate-strip` (section 4) is the strongest visual: year-by-year bars from 1950 to present with La Niña years colour-coded in blue but still deeply in the orange/red zone of the chart. Designer needs the year-by-year temperature anomaly dataset — the Carbon Brief State of Climate articles cover each year back to 2014 and Copernicus ERA5 provides the full historical series.
- The `data-readout` (section 3) naturally becomes a 5-row table (2022–2026) with three columns: year, anomaly (°C above pre-industrial), and ENSO state. Simplest possible illustration of the staircase in numbers.
