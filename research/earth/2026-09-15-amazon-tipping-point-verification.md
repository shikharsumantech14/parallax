**Where each band actually reaches the reader** — and one correction to the
brief. The bar **labels carry the conditions**, not the bands ("If the cutting
stays where it is" / "If the cutting goes on"). The bands are in the
`sublabel`s, which render nothing, and in the `caption`, which renders at the
top of the card. So:

- **3.7–4°C** reaches the reader three times: §1's caption, §1's `actually.value`
  printed large, §2's caption. Robust.
- **22–28%** reaches the reader twice: §2's caption and §4's zone legend chip
  (which is what moved all four panel readers from "a bit further along" to "22").
- **1.5–1.9°C** reaches the reader **once**, in §2's caption, and nowhere else.
  That satisfies the rule, and it is worth the operator knowing it is
  single-stranded: if that caption were ever trimmed, the upper bound of the
  issue's most important band would leave the page silently, because the
  sublabel that appears to carry it renders nothing. Not a defect today.

The `plain` lines state the rule in the reader's words in both places ("Both
bars are drawn at the low end of their published range" / "The arc is drawn at
the lower of the two published readings"), and panel 1 recorded that the hero's
version "reads as careful". The device works.

**The one place the conservative reading is not taken is the hook**, which pairs
4 (high end) with 1.5 (low end) — see judgement call 1.

### 3. THE BARRED IMPLICATION — **it does not form. Audited as a factual matter, independently of the panels.**

**No sentence in this issue, read alone or with its neighbour, asserts or
implies that Amazon clearing changes Indian rainfall.** I tested five paths:

1. **The borrowing sentence alone.** *"The Amazon's rain starts the way the
   monsoon does, and you already know that first half."* The grammatical subject
   is **the Amazon's rain**; the monsoon is the object of a comparison of
   mechanism. No causal verb connects them in either direction. "That first
   half" fences the transfer in advance, inside the tempting sentence.
2. **With its neighbour, both ways.** Preceding: §2's intro ends on savanna in
   the basin. Following: *"But in the Amazon the forest itself keeps that air
   wet."* The contrast relocates the mechanism explicitly. Neither neighbour
   supplies a causal link.
3. **The skim path (title + first line).** *"Cutting here kills trees over
   there"* + the borrowing sentence. The title's object is **trees**, and this
   issue contains no Indian trees, no Indian forest and no Indian rainfall
   anywhere — so the leap requires inventing a noun the text does not supply.
4. **Cards only.** Step 2 says "in parts of the basin"; step 3 says "hundreds of
   kilometres away". Both locate the mechanism inside the Amazon.
5. **Forward through the issue.** "Monsoon" occurs exactly once in the file.
   India occurs once more, as **Delhi**, and only as a unit of area with its
   basis printed. The two never meet.

The remaining risk is a reader who imports a belief they already held. The text
does not supply it, does not confirm it, and gives it no hook. **The bar holds.
No change wanted.**

### 6. Copied figures, and the unit mismatch — **ruling: leaving it unconverted is honest**

Every figure in the §8e ledger's "copied" list is present and correct: `1.5`,
`1.9`, `3.7`, `4`, `17`, `18`, `22`, `28`, `50`, `2030`, `5,796`, `11`, `2014`,
`31`, `20`, `25`, `2.78`, `60`, `31.3`, `50.8`, `91`, `36`, `2040`, `2018`,
`2024`, `2025`, `2026` and every date. Both of the ledger's explicit watch items
survived. The en-dashes inside ranges are en-dashes throughout, and the comma in
`5,796` is intact.

**On 2.78 million hectares beside 5,796 km² — leave it unconverted.** Three
reasons, in the order they matter:

1. **They measure different things**, and converting would invite exactly the
   false comparison the issue is trying to prevent. 2.78 M ha is primary-forest
   **loss** (the Global Forest Watch measure, majority fire); 5,796 km² is PRODES
   **clearing**. Put them in one unit side by side and the reader gets an
   apparently clean "2024 was 4.8 times worse than 2025", which is not a claim
