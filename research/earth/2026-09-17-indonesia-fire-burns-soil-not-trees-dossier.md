## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The reader arrives believing Indonesia's fire season is a forest fire — trees
burning, smoke rising, an El Niño drought to blame, and an area of scorched
land that tells you how bad it was. Every part of that is the wrong frame. The
fuel that matters is not the forest but the ground underneath it: waterlogged
peat, which is the undecomposed remains of thousands of years of plant matter,
holding roughly 28 billion tonnes of carbon in Indonesia alone. Peat cannot
burn while it is wet. It burns because plantation drainage canals were dug to
lower the water table so oil palm and pulpwood roots would not drown — legally
to 40 cm, in practice to 50–75 cm — and dry peat is fuel that burns downward,
smouldering, sometimes for months. That is why the arithmetic of this fire
refuses to behave: in 2015 about 333 million tonnes of carbon came out of an
area that is a rounding error on the map of Indonesia, roughly 38% of India's
entire annual CO2 output from a country whose own fossil emissions are a fifth
of India's. The mechanism that creates the gap between surface and structure
is depth — burned *area* is a two-dimensional measure of a three-dimensional
fire, and the missing dimension is 24.8 cm of carbon-dense soil that took
centuries to accumulate and left in a single October. The same drainage
releases roughly 72 MtC a year with no fire at all. And the agency built
specifically to re-wet that peat was allowed to lapse at the end of December
2024 and was folded away in 2025, which means the plumbing that makes this
possible is now less supervised than it was, not more.

## 2. Why now

Indonesia's 2026 fire season is running hot in an El Niño-adjacent year and
the numbers landed in the last five weeks:

- **11 September 2026** — Carbon Brief published its analysis of Indonesian
  fire carbon emissions for the season to date: **76 MtC emitted by 7
  September 2026**, already approaching the **~85 MtC** 2000–2026 full-season
  average, and the highest since 2015's 333 MtC.
  [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-indonesia-fire-emissions-2026/)
- **24 August 2026 (reported 25 Aug)** — Indonesia's burned area for the year
  reached **286,000 ha**, and **72 people had been charged** with fire-related
  offences.
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/08/indonesia-fires-burned-area-charges/)
- **4 September 2026** — **50,891 cases of acute respiratory infection (ISPA)**
  reported in the worst-hit provinces.
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/09/indonesia-haze-respiratory-cases/)
- **The structural anchor:** the peat restoration agency's mandate **lapsed on
  31 December 2024** and the body was dissolved during 2025, so the 2026 season
  is the first full fire season with no dedicated national re-wetting agency.

The "why now" the issue should lead with is the **76 MtC by 7 September**
figure, because it is the number that makes no sense next to 286,000 ha — and
that mismatch *is* the issue.

### 4b. Depth — the missing dimension

- **Mean peat burn depth, Oct 2015:** **24.8 cm** (Aug 15.0 cm → Sep 23.6 cm →
  Oct 24.8 cm) — [Kiely et al. 2019, ACP](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/19/11105/2019/)
- **Legal "sustainable" water table:** **40 cm** below surface —
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/peat-water-table-regulation/)
- **Actual oil-palm drainage depth:** **50–75 cm** —
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/peat-water-table-regulation/)
  — *drawing rule: draw the low end, 50 cm; the band goes in the caption*
- **Peat thickness in Indonesia:** **0.5 m to more than 20 m** —
  [WRI](https://www.wri.org/insights/peatlands-explained)
- **Surface fuel must burn first:** peat ignition in Indonesian peatland is
  preceded by combustion of the surface litter/vegetation layer; the peat
  itself then smoulders — [Yokelson et al. 2022, ACP](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/10173/2022/)

**`three-steps`** — `{ steps: [{title ≤6 words, text ≤25 words}] }`
