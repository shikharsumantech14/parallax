## The hook

Stopped the thumb for all four, with one caveat. "You have seen the burned-area figure: 286,000 hectares. It misses the part that matters. In October 2015 this fire ate 24.8 centimetres of ground." carries a concrete number, direct address ("You have seen"), and a twist ("misses the part that matters") — the three things the contract asks for, and Aarav, Meera and Karthik all named the 24.8 cm line as what made them keep scrolling. Sana said yes but "barely" — the hook reads as explainer-voice rather than a vivid image; it has the number but nothing she could picture in a thumbnail (smoke, a collapsing patch of ground) the way the later "school ruler" and "tap left running" lines do. It works; it is the least memorable hook the panel has seen from this desk.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 7 (power-flow):** "multiplying by 12 over 44" arrives with no reason given — gloss why the chart is converting CO2 to carbon in the same breath, or move the mechanics of the conversion to the source line and let the caption carry only the comparison the reader needs.
2. **Section 7 (power-flow):** the fuller answer to "how big next to India" — that Indonesia's own fossil emissions (221) are a fraction of India's (870) even though the fire alone hits 38% — is only visible as two bar widths. Say it as a sentence; right now every reader has to do that division themselves, and none of the four did.
3. **Section 1 (benchmark-chart):** "The 1997 bar is drawn at 800, and the record says just over that" reads as an aside about how the chart was drawn rather than a fact about 1997. State the 1997 figure as the plain fact ("in 1997 the fires put out just over 800 million tonnes") and let the caption's job stay data, not drafting notes.
