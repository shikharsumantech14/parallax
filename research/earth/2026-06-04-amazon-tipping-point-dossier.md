## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The climate conversation treats the Amazon as a thermometer problem: hold
global warming under 1.5°C and the rainforest survives. A 7 May 2026
*Nature* paper (Wunderling et al.) inverts the controls. It maps **two
different tipping thresholds against two different dials.** Turn only the
temperature dial — leave clearing where it is today — and the forest does not
collapse until **3.7–4°C** of warming, a level no realistic pathway reaches
this century. Turn the *deforestation* dial instead — push clearing from
today's **~17–18%** up to **22–28%** of the forest — and the tipping point
drops all the way down to **1.5–1.9°C**, a warming band the world is on track
to touch as early as **2030**. The two curves cross because of a mechanism
the temperature framing ignores: the forest manufactures up to **half its own
rainfall**, so each hectare cleared dries the air for forest hundreds of
kilometres downwind, and the drought stress kills trees that were never cut.
The revelation for the reader: the lever that actually decides whether the
Amazon survives is not the global thermostat that takes every nation a
century to move — it is a clearing rate that **one government (Brazil) can
move in a single budget cycle.** The headline number everyone watches (the
warming target) is not the number the forest is most sensitive to. The
framing hides the choice; the paper names it.

## 2. Why now

A peer-reviewed paper in *Nature*, published **7 May 2026** —
"Deforestation-induced drying lowers Amazon climate threshold" — led by
**Nico Wunderling** (Goethe University Frankfurt / Potsdam Institute for
Climate Impact Research). It is the first to model the Amazon tipping point
with **both** deforestation *and* warming as independent inputs and quantify
how much each lowers the threshold for the other. Its finding: with continued
clearing, more than two-thirds of the rainforest could flip toward savanna
**as early as the 2040s**.

- **Carbon Brief** carried it in the **8 May 2026 DeBriefed** with the
  threshold figures quoted directly:
  [Carbon Brief, 8 May 2026](https://www.carbonbrief.org/debriefed-8-may-2026-eu-eyes-fossil-fuel-exemptions-wind-and-solar-save-uk-1-7bn-amazon-tipping-point/)
- **Mongabay** ran a full write-up the same week (7 May 2026):
  [Mongabay, 7 May 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/05/deforestation-and-warming-could-push-amazon-to-tipping-point-by-2040s-study/)

The "why now" is reinforced by a near-simultaneous data backdrop: the WMO's
28 May 2026 decadal update puts a **91% chance** the world breaches 1.5°C in
at least one year before 2030, and Carbon Brief's analysis puts the central
estimate for crossing 1.5°C "around 2030" — i.e. the world is arriving at the
exact warming band where, *if clearing also continues*, the forest tips.

### The mechanism (hydrological self-destruction)

- **Self-recycled rainfall:** "Up to **half** of the Amazon Rainforest's
  precipitation is recycled by its trees"; "for parts of the rainforest, more
  than **50%** of its rainfall is generated by the forest itself."
  Corroborated by both, and by a 2022 Carbon Brief explainer.
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/05/deforestation-and-warming-could-push-amazon-to-tipping-point-by-2040s-study/) ·
  [Carbon Brief, 8 May 2026](https://www.carbonbrief.org/debriefed-8-may-2026-eu-eyes-fossil-fuel-exemptions-wind-and-solar-save-uk-1-7bn-amazon-tipping-point/) ·
  [Carbon Brief, 7 Mar 2022](https://www.carbonbrief.org/declining-resilience-pushing-amazon-rainforest-towards-tipping-point/)
  ("The Amazon rainforest sustains its own climate by recycling water to the
  atmosphere")
- **Downwind kill:** "when moisture transport is disrupted in one area, the
  resulting drought stress can kill trees **hundreds of kilometers away**."
  [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/05/deforestation-and-warming-could-push-amazon-to-tipping-point-by-2040s-study/)
