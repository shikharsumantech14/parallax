**Karthik's Hindi audit — the one this panel exists to run.** Two Hindi
strings reach the page.

- `garam saal, thanda saal` (§3, jargon-buster). **Passes the skip test.** The
  English line directly above it says "the Pacific's warm phase and its cool
  phase". Delete the Hindi and Karthik loses nothing. Not load-bearing.
- `jhoola` (the dek). **Fails the skip test inside its own line.** Strip it and
  the dek reads "A staircase, not a — and it only goes up": the thing being
  corrected disappears, and with it the reversal that is the dek's whole job.
  Karthik does recover the picture one section later, where the `you-think`
  panel says "Like a swing" in English — so the *issue* does not go dark, and
  this is not a block. But for the length of the head, the correction is
  carried by a word he cannot read.

No other section depends on a Hindi word, and none appears in a caption,
a plain line, a how-to-read or a source line.

## The hook

> "You have lived through the eleven hottest years ever recorded. The last of
> them, 2025, was supposed to be a cool one."

**It stops the thumb for all four.** "You have lived through" does the work the
contract asks of it, and "supposed to be" plants the twist without spending the
issue's one reversal.

- **Aarav — yes.** He can repeat it back verbatim after one read. He wants to
  know which eleven years; §6's last tile answers it later.
- **Meera — yes, and it pays off immediately.** §1 is the payoff, one scroll
  down. She does not leave.
- **Sana — yes.** It is about her, and it has a number she can hold. What it
  lacks is a thing to picture: eleven years is a count, not an image. The
  staircase, which is the issue's picture, is in the dek beside it and in the
  last paragraph, but never in the hook.
- **Karthik — yes.** No Hindi, no unglossed term, one claim per sentence.

The title states the finding and needs no second reading from anyone.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **§8: the +2.2°C tile is a fourth kind of degree and nothing says so.** The
   reader has just held 1.44, 1.55 and 1.47, all measured from 1850–1900;
   +2.2 is a Pacific patch against its own normal. Aarav asked "of the world?"
   out loud. Give the tile's note the baseline the way the other tiles have
   one, or move the figure out of the grid so it cannot be read down the
   column with the global anomalies.
2. **§8: the Arctic sea-ice tile answers none of the three questions** — and it
   is carrying the issue's only Indian comparison. Either give the ice a job in
   the argument, or move the "4.3 times India" scale onto a number that does
   work (the ocean's heat gain in §4 is the candidate the reader already met).
3. **Question 3's answer is printed once, at the bottom, in the only text-only
   block a phone reader skims.** "2027" needs to sit on something that cannot be
   scrolled past — the lag tile in §8 or §9's own title — so Sana leaves with
   the year and not just "later".
4. **§6: the title promises two hot years and the grid shows one.** Karthik
   counted; Meera re-read. Either the title matches the tiles or a second peak
   tile joins them.
5. **§6: "What La Niña pulled off 2025"** is read as an achievement, not a
   subtraction, by two of the four. The label needs the direction in it.
6. **The glossary spends its three slots on terms the primer already glossed,
   and misses the two words the graphics actually use** — "anomaly" (§2's
   caption, the hero) and "trough" (§7's title). "Floor" is a third: it is the
   issue's argument (§5, §9) and appears only as metaphor.
7. **The hero has no restatement after it.** §3 opens on housekeeping ("the
   rest of the issue leans on these three"), so the strip's finding is left to
   the in-graphic callouts alone. The seam rule wants the next sentence the
   reader meets to say what the strip showed, in their words.
8. **§2's intro sends the reader to "the dips" before anything has said the
   strip carries a line.** The plain line that explains the form sits *below*
   the graphic; the instruction sits above it.
9. **§3's intro says "so here they are first"** in section 3 of 9. Small, but
   it is the first sentence Meera argued with.
10. **⚠ verify (not my gate, flagged and moving on):** §6 says 2024 was the
    first year above 1.5°C, and §8 gives 2026 a ~30% chance of "exceeding
    1.5°C". Read together they contradict; the distinction between a single
    year over the line and the threshold itself is never stated on the page.
    Whether the numbers are right is the verifier's call — that they read as a
    contradiction is mine.
