## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The reader arrives thinking this is a viral culture-war episode: a judge
insulted unemployed youth, the internet made a joke party called itself
after the insult, the government banned it, and a meme became a martyr. That
framing treats the ban as the *end* of the story — the state's brake on a
runaway account. The structural truth is the inverse. A national takedown
order is a **centralized instrument aimed at a borderless object**, and in
this case the act of suppression is what manufactured the reach it meant to
deny. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) account lived on US-domiciled
platforms (Instagram, X), was run by a founder physically in Boston, and
crossed roughly 20 million followers in under a week. India's blocking
machinery — Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, executed
through confidential orders that the platform must obey but cannot disclose
even to a court — can compel a US platform to *withhold* an account inside
India's borders. It cannot delete the account, cannot reach the servers,
cannot touch the followers who already screenshotted every post, and cannot
stop the same content reappearing on a backup handle. So the §69A order did
two things at once: it removed the account from one jurisdiction and it
*certified the account as dangerous enough to ban* to every other
jurisdiction on Earth. The mechanism that creates the gap between surface
("ban = silence") and structure ("ban = amplification") is the mismatch
between a **territorial legal tool** and a **non-territorial target** — the
Streisand effect, but operating at the scale of a 1.4-billion-person state
acting against an object it does not actually control. The issue's job is to
map the follower curve against the state-action timeline so the reader can
see the amplification happen, then expose the censorship architecture
underneath so they understand *why* a national ban is structurally
incapable of silencing a diaspora-hosted account — and what that says about
the limits of §69A as a speech-control instrument in 2026.

## 2. Why now

The episode is roughly three weeks old and still live in the courts as of
the research date. The hard, datable anchor chain:

- **15 May 2026** — CJI Surya Kant makes the "cockroaches / parasites"
  remark in open court.
- **16 May 2026** — CJI issues a clarification; the same day, the Cockroach
  Janta Party is founded as a satirical response.
- **21 May 2026** — MeitY orders X to withhold the CJP account under §69A.
- **23 May 2026** — the CJP website is taken down and the founder says he has
  lost access to all platforms.
- **29 May 2026** — Delhi High Court refuses interim relief and refers the
  block to the MeitY Review Committee; **matter posted for 7 July 2026.**

The freshest hard peg for the hero is the **29 May 2026 Delhi High Court
order** (refusing to restore the account, sending it to the Review
Committee, next hearing 7 July) — a live judicial proceeding, which is the
strongest "why now" because it is dated, official, and ongoing. The "why
now" feeds the timeline's final shipped event; the 7 July hearing is the
open question to gesture at.

context (which The Wire explainer covers). Do not conflate them.
