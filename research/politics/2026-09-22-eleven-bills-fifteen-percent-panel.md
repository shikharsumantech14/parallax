## The hook

*"Your MP's House sat 15% of its scheduled time. It still passed eleven of twelve bills. Nine had no speaker but the minister."*

- **Aarav — yes.** "Your MP" plus the flat numbers is enough; a bank clerk who reads headlines gets the shape of the surprise immediately.
- **Meera — yes, with a stumble.** The twist ("still passed eleven of twelve") pays off fast, but "Nine had no speaker but the minister" costs her a re-parse before the third sentence lands.
- **Sana — yes, but weakly.** It has her "you" (her MP) and a twist, but nothing to *picture* — three sentences of pure count-and-percent, no image the way "your place in the line" worked in the contract's worked example. It would stop her thumb, not linger there.
- **Karthik — yes.** Clean, literal, no Hindi to parse — the "but" construction is the only friction, and it is mild.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 4's intro drops an orphan stat.** "Seven of these bills took five minutes or less" arrives with no row in the four-measure card that shows it — connect it to one of the four rows (or the debate-time theme in section 2/5), or cut it.
2. **Question 3 stops one clause short of its own storyboard warning.** The draft states plainly that the government controls *prorogation* (section 8's quote, section 9's intro) but never states, anywhere, that the government also controls *committee referral* and *summoning* — the "calendar the House does not set" in section 6 needs the "who" named once, in the same section that already carries the Article 85 source line.
3. **"Speaker" (a person who spoke) and "the Speaker" (the presiding officer) share one word across the hook, section 2 and section 9.** On a phone mid-scroll the capital letter is easy to miss; reword one sense so the two roles don't collide.
4. **Section 9's first event (17 April, the 131st Amendment Bill) has no stated connection to the throughline** before it. Either bridge it in one clause or reconsider whether it needs to open the timeline at all.
