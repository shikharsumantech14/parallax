## The hook

> "One MP in Uttar Pradesh speaks for 30 lakh people. In Tamil Nadu, 18 lakh.
> The bill to close that gap lost by 54 votes."

- **Aarav — yes.** His state, his number, and a defeat in twenty-four words.
- **Meera — yes.** The contrast is crisp and "lost by 54 votes" is the
  cliffhanger that buys the next screen.
- **Sana — yes, weakly.** It stops the thumb on the arithmetic, not on her.
  There is no "you" and nothing she owns; she is watching two MPs, not
  herself. This is the measured cost of the operator's ruling (storyboard §9.2)
  that dropped the second person — reported, not contested.
- **Karthik — yes, strongest of the four.** Tamil Nadu is in the second
  sentence. He is the most invested reader in the issue by the end of line two.

**What it lacks:** a thing to picture. It carries two numbers and no image —
30 lakh is stated, never made physical, in the hook or in §1's `equals` column.
The storyboard ruled out a city-sized comparison for want of a source
(§5, "Not used, deliberately"), so this is a known, accepted gap rather than
an oversight. Sana is where the cost shows.

## Why REVISE and not PASS

By the letter of the gate this is a PASS on three of four criteria: every
question is answered correctly by Aarav, Meera and Sana; every section scores
≥ 4 for those three; and no section is ≤ 2 for anyone.

It fails the fourth. **One Hindi phrase is load-bearing** — not inside its
section, where the intro, the quote and the followup carry the meaning and
Karthik retold it correctly, but as a **section title**. A title is the one
place where the English beside it is not guaranteed to travel with it: the
issue page's aside renders a contents list of section titles, where
"Credit nahi chahiye." stands alone. Apply the skip test to the title as a
unit and what survives is "Credit." — which says nothing. That is the finding
this panel exists to make, and it is the operator's call to take or leave
(storyboard §8c.3 and §9.3 already flag it as reversible).

Everything else on this list is mechanical: a sentence that joins two numbers,
a gloss on one word, a glyph, and a prop the dispatcher does not pass.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **§6 · the hero is counting a house the issue never named.** Sections 2 and
   4 put the Lok Sabha at 850; the chart's subtitle says "543-seat scenario"
   and nothing joins them. Both readers outside the Hindi belt answered
   question 2 correctly and then said they could not tell whether Tamil Nadu
   loses eight real seats or eight shares of a bigger house. The hero's intro
   is where that sentence belongs — say which house the change column counts,
   before the reader meets the column.
2. **§6 and §8 · the authored captions never render.** `SectionBody` passes no
   `caption` to `seat-chart` or `vote-result`. Either wire the prop through
   both arms, or move each restatement into the section's `intro` — but the
   post-graphic restatement must exist somewhere on the page, and right now
   the issue's two load-bearing ones do not.
3. **§2 and §4 · 543, 550 and 850 arrive as three unrelated numbers.** The
   reader meets "543 → 850", then "the cap from 550 to 850" two sections later,
   with nothing distinguishing seats filled from the constitutional ceiling.
   Separate the two at the first place both appear, or use one of them
   throughout.
4. **§9 · "Credit nahi chahiye." is the only line Karthik cannot read, and it
   is a title.** Either put the English in the title and keep the Hindi where
   the quote's English sits beside it, or accept the cost knowingly — it is an
   operator ruling, not a drafting error. Note also that the quote is ellipsed
   at the head, so the words the title renders are nowhere on the page.
5. **§1 · the `≈` glyph promises an equivalence and delivers a contrast.** All
   four readers re-read the first `equals` row; 30 lakh is not approximately
   18 lakh. The row's own wording has to carry the comparison so the glyph
   cannot mislead — or the contrast belongs in the `note`, where the second
   row's "12 lakh more people" already does the job well.
6. **§1 · "about six-tenths of a Tamil Nadu vote" is a ratio with nothing
   beside it.** Aarav's first re-read. The row above it has a `note` doing
   exactly the felt-comparison job; this one has none.
7. **§3 · two small holes the readers found.** The intro says the map has not
   moved since 1976 while the first event dates the last full redraw to 1971;
   and "Three bills, one special session" never names the other two, which
   §8's "the two companion bills fell with it" then calls back to. ⚠ verify
   both — the panel reports the reader's stumble, not the fact.
8. **§5 · the analogy opens cold.** No intro, no column labels, and a headline
   whose "house" means both the Lok Sabha and the family. Every reader got
   there, none on the first row. A label on the two columns would pay for
   itself.
