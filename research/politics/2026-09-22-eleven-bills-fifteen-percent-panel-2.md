## The hook

*"Your MP's House sat 15% of its scheduled time. It still passed eleven of
twelve bills. Nine had no speaker but the minister."*

- **Aarav — yes.** "Your MP" personalises it, three numbers land clean, and
  the twist (barely worked, still legislated) is the whole issue in one
  breath.
- **Meera — yes.** It pays off immediately in row 1, which is what keeps her
  past the first paragraph.
- **Sana — borderline / no.** It has the number and the "you", but no image
  to picture — a scheduled-time percentage and a bill count are a stat, not
  a scene. On a feed she scrolls past a stat unless it is attached to
  something she can see (an empty chair, a locked door, a clock). This is
  the one persona for whom the hook does not clearly stop the thumb.
- **Karthik — yes.** The counter-intuitive numeric anomaly (low input, high
  output) is exactly the kind of thing an engineer's eye catches mid-scroll.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Row 6 or row 8: name the referral decision, not just the calendar.**
   Every persona answers Q3 "partly" for the same reason — the draft states
   clearly that the government controls *when* a session ends and *how much
   time* a calendar gives a committee, but never states that referring a
   bill to a committee at all is itself the government's call, independent
   of time. One added clause in row 6's second card, or the quote's
   follow-up sentence, closing that gap would move Q3 from partly to
   correct for all three readers.
2. **Row 5: the "failed" status on the committee-referral bar needs its
   gloss earlier, not only in the trailing note.** A reader meets "failed"
   before "not referred" and briefly reads it as "attempted and rejected."
   Move the sense of "never entered" into the label itself or the first
   clause the reader meets, not just the note that follows it.
3. **Row 7's intro: "that gap did not open this year" is compressed past
   the reader who hasn't seen the chart yet.** Name what the gap is (the
   distance between a same-day vote and a multi-week committee stage) in
   the same clause, rather than trusting the chart below to resolve it a
   beat later.
4. **The hook, for the social-scroll reader specifically:** it has a number
   and a "you" but no picture. One concrete thing to see (what an MP's
   empty debate chair looks like; what fifteen minutes out of a hundred
   feels like) alongside the stat would make it stop a thumb that is not
   already inclined toward parliamentary procedure.
