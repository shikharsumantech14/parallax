## C-01 · The Vote That Rewrote the Map — and Then Didn't

- **status:** open
- **why now:** On April 17, 2026, the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill — the
  Modi government's three-bill package to expand the Lok Sabha to 850 seats and
  trigger delimitation — was defeated in Lok Sabha. It received 298 votes against
  the 352 needed for a two-thirds majority; the government fell 54 votes short.
  This is the first time a Modi-era constitutional amendment has failed to pass.
  The Delimitation Bill was subsequently withdrawn on the same day.
- **angle:** Parallax reveals the arithmetic trap the government walked into: to
  amend the Constitution you need two-thirds of members present and voting, but
  every regional party whose state stood to lose seats under a population-proportional
  reapportionment had a structural incentive to oppose regardless of coalition
  loyalty. Tamil Nadu (39 → 32 seats), Kerala (20 → 15), Andhra Pradesh (42 → 38)
  versus Uttar Pradesh (80 → 89), Bihar (40 → 46) — the seat-redistribution map
  IS the vote-coalition map. Parallax shows why the two numbers are the same number.

## C-02 · The Machine That Deleted 91 Lakh Voters

- **status:** queued-next
- **why now:** West Bengal's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls,
  conducted October 2025–April 2026, removed approximately 91 lakh names from
  the voter list before the April 23 & 29 assembly elections — an 11.6% reduction
  in the state's electorate. Only 136 of 27 lakh appeals were restored before
  the first polling phase. Results came in on May 4: TMC won 215 of 294 seats
  despite the controversy, but the structural question about the SIR's design
  persists nationally.
- **angle:** Parallax shows this is not a Bengal story but a systems story: the SIR
  is a nation-wide procedural mechanism (it also ran in Assam, Tamil Nadu, Kerala,
  Puducherry, and earlier in 16+ other states including UP where 2.04 crore names
  were deleted). The structural revelation is that the SIR places the burden of
  proof on the voter — not the state — and the appeals pipeline was designed at
  a scale (27 lakh appeals, 136 restored) that makes meaningful remedy structurally
  impossible. The Parallax frame: a democratic system that procedurally automates
  disenfranchisement faster than it can adjudicate appeals.

## C-03 · The Protection Act That Criminalized Identity

- **status:** chosen
- **why now:** The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Bill, 2026
  was passed by Lok Sabha on March 24 and Rajya Sabha on March 25, with presidential
  assent on March 30. It amends the 2019 Act by removing self-perceived gender
  identity, replacing it with a mandatory District Magistrate certificate issued
  after a government medical board review. It introduces life imprisonment for
  "coercing or alluring" someone to assume a transgender identity.
- **angle:** The structural revelation: the 2019 Act was itself already a retreat
  from the 2014 Supreme Court NALSA ruling that established self-perceived gender
  identity as a constitutional right. The 2026 Amendment is a second retreat — a
  ratchet moving in one direction. Parallax traces the arc from NALSA 2014 →
  Transgender Act 2019 → Amendment 2026, showing how each legislative cycle
  encoded more state gatekeeping into what the court originally declared a
  fundamental right. The law that was named "Protection" now includes provisions
  a Supreme Court-appointed expert committee explicitly asked the government to
  withdraw.

## C-04 · The Number India Hasn't Counted Since 1931

- **status:** open
- **why now:** On April 30, 2025, the Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs approved
  including caste enumeration in the 2027 Census — the first national-level caste
  count since 1931. The House Listing phase of the Census commenced April 1, 2026.
  MoSPI released its "Women and Men in India 2025" data report on April 29, 2026,
  foregrounding the absence of granular population data as a governance gap.
- **angle:** India's 27% OBC reservation quota — which governs access to government
  jobs and university seats for hundreds of millions — rests on a 1980 Mandal
  Commission estimate that itself drew on 1931 census figures. No nationally
  counted OBC population number has existed since independence. The structural
  revelation: every legal challenge to the 50% reservation cap, every sub-quota
  dispute, every claim of "creamy layer" has been litigated on top of a data void.
  The 2027 caste census is not a policy — it is the first time the state will know
  what it has been governing. Parallax traces the 95-year data absence and its
  downstream legal and political effects.
- **suggested kinds:** hero, timeline, data-readout, analogy, paradox, prose
