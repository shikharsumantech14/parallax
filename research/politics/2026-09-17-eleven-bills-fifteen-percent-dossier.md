## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The reader arrives believing that a Parliament which does not function does
not legislate: shouting, placards, adjournment within minutes, nothing
passed. The Monsoon Session of 2026 says the opposite, and the two halves of
the sentence are the same fact. The Lok Sabha worked 15% of its scheduled
time and passed eleven of the twelve bills before it. Nine of those bills
cleared the House without a single MP other than the piloting minister
speaking, seven in five minutes or less, and not one of the eleven had been
examined by a parliamentary committee first. The gap between surface and
structure is this: **passing a bill costs a vote, examining one costs
time — and the side that owns the clock is not the side that wants the
examination.** The government fixes when Parliament meets (Article 85), the
government decides whether a bill goes to a committee, and the government
decides when a session formally ends. So a collapse of time does not slow
legislation down; it removes the only stage of lawmaking that time protects.
The session that adjourned on 13 August and has still not been prorogued
five weeks later is the same instrument read from the other end: a session
kept technically alive is a House that can be recalled with its business
intact. Output was never the thing under strain. Examination was, and it is
not measured by any number the House publishes about itself.

## 2. Why now

- The Monsoon Session ran **20 July to 13 August 2026**, 19 sittings, and
  adjourned *sine die* on 13 August.
  ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/vital-stats))
- PRS's session vital statistics record the **Lok Sabha functioning for 15%
  of scheduled time and the Rajya Sabha for 33%**, with Question Hour at
  **1% (LS) and 12% (RS)** — the Lok Sabha's lowest in about a decade.
- **Twelve bills introduced, eleven passed both Houses**; the single
  survivor is the Indian Statistical Institute Bill, 2026, which went to a
  committee.
  ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/bill-legislation))
- **Nine of the passed bills cleared the Lok Sabha with no MP other than the
  minister speaking** (Newslaundry, 20 Aug 2026); **seven of eleven passed in
  five minutes or less** (PTI via ThePrint, 22 Aug 2026).
- The live hook: as of **14 September 2026 the session had still not been
  prorogued — 32 days after adjournment**, against a Rajya Sabha manual
  practice of two to ten days.
  ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/adjourned-in-august-monsoon-session-yet-to-be-prorogued-32-days-later-what-this-means))
  As of the research date (17 September 2026) no prorogation notification was
  found; **the issue must re-check this on the publication date.**

### 4.3 The calendar as an instrument (T0 anchor)

- **Article 85(1), verbatim:** "The President shall from time to time summon
  each House of Parliament to meet at such time and place as he thinks fit,
  but six months shall not intervene between its last sitting in one session
  and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session." —
  [Constitution of India, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India/Part_V)
- **Article 85(2), verbatim:** "The President may from time to time—(a)
  prorogue the Houses or either House; (b) dissolve the House of the People."
  — same source.
- **Article 123(1), verbatim:** "If at any time, except when both Houses of
  Parliament are in session, the President is satisfied that circumstances
  exist which render it necessary for him to take immediate action, he may
  promulgate such Ordinances as the circumstances appear to him to require."
  — same source. **This is the hinge:** a House that is not prorogued is
  still "in session", so the ordinance route is shut while it stays open.
- **Who actually decides:** "The power to summon parliament rests with the
  executive"; the Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs fixes the dates
  and the President is then advised. — [The Wire (2017)](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/parliament-doesnt-power-convene-effective-can-really)
- **The prorogation norm:** the Rajya Sabha manual records the interval
  between adjournment *sine die* and prorogation as varying "between 2 and
  10 days". 32 days had passed by 14 September 2026. — [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/adjourned-in-august-monsoon-session-yet-to-be-prorogued-32-days-later-what-this-means)
- **The delimitation context (use once, dated and attributed — do not
  re-explain delimitation; it is told in `2026-04-24-delimitation`):** the
  Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 was introduced 16 April 2026 and
  **negatived 17 April 2026**; it would have raised the Lok Sabha's maximum
  from 550 to 850 seats and allowed delimitation on the 2011 census. —
  [PRS Bill Track](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026)

### 4.4 Derived numbers (the issue may use these; each is arithmetic on a
sourced figure, and the derivation must be stated)

- **15% of a 19-sitting session = the equivalent of about 2.9 sitting days
  of work.** Derived: 19 × 0.15. Basis: PRS's 15%.
- **1% of Question Hour = about 36 seconds of the scheduled hour.** Derived:
  1% × 60 minutes. Basis: PRS's 1%; Question Hour is the first hour of a
  sitting. **Do not state it as an exact daily measurement** — PRS gives a
  session-wide percentage, not a per-day stopwatch.
- **15% of a scheduled hour ≈ 9 minutes.** Derived the same way.
- **The 15th Lok Sabha's 71% referral rate applied to this session's 11
  passed bills = about 8 bills.** Derived. Used only as the "requirement"
  mark in `margin-bullets` §4.5(B), and must be labelled as a comparison,
  never as a rule.
- **9 of 11 passed bills had no non-minister speaker, so 2 did.** Derived
  subtraction from the Newslaundry / Bhanu figure.
- **55 sitting days a year is 46% of the 120 the constitutional review
  commission recommended.** Derived.

**[UNVERIFIED] items the editor must rule on before the draft.**

1. **Presidential assent to the eleven bills is not confirmed.** PRS's
   legislation tracker records passage by both Houses, not assent. The issue
   must say "passed" rather than "became law", or the drafter must verify
   assent on India Code (`indiacode.nic.in`, T0, on the allowlist) for any
   bill it calls an Act. The `bill-passage` assent row is marked `pending`
   for this reason.
2. **Prorogation status on the publication date.** Everything in §4.3 rests
   on The Wire's 14 September count of 32 days. The House may be prorogued
   between research and publication, which would date the issue's sharpest
   live fact. **Re-check before publishing and date the figure explicitly
   ("as of ...").** Sansad.in does not expose this; the check is a fresh
   search of the allowlisted outlets.
3. **A genuine conflict on committee referral during this session.** PRS's
   legislation table marks the Indian Statistical Institute Bill, 2026
   (introduced 3 August) as referred to a committee. Newslaundry reports
   "zero newly referred during the Monsoon Session" and two referrals in
   2026 overall. Both cannot be right. The dossier resolves it the narrow
   way — **none of the eleven bills that PASSED had been to a committee**,
   which both sources support — and the issue should make that the claim.
   Do not write "no bill was referred".
4. **"12 bills passed" appears in general coverage and is not PRS's count.**
   Several outlets say the Lok Sabha passed twelve bills; PRS counts eleven
   passed by both Houses plus one pending, and excludes the Appropriation
   Bill from its legislation count. Use PRS's count and state the definition
   once. The two numbers are not a contradiction, they are two denominators.
5. **OpIndia's figures could not be verified.** A search summary attributed
   19% (Lok Sabha) and 39% (Rajya Sabha) productivity and a "25-day session"

   government explanation is on record in any source found.

**One honest tension the issue should not hide.** The 17th Lok Sabha
