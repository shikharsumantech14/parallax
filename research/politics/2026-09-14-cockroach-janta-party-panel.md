## The hook

> "Twenty million followers in a week. Then one order made the account vanish
> from your phone, and from nobody else's."

**Sana — yes, it stops her thumb.** A follower count is her native unit and
"your phone" is a thing she is holding while she reads it. The twist lands in
the last four words. She would keep scrolling into §1.

**Two things it lacks, neither of which is the one the regex flagged.**

1. **"Twenty million" spelled out lands softer than a digit would**, for her
   specifically. Prose-set numerals read as words and get scanned; a digit
   string is the thing the eye catches mid-scroll. And the unit she actually
   counts in — **two crore** — is in the issue but nowhere in the head, saved
   for §5 and §6. "2 crore followers in a week" would stop her harder than
   "Twenty million" does, and it would place the issue in India in the first
   four words. This is a judgement about the reader, not about the gate: the
   line does not fail, it under-performs its own material.
2. **"The account" has no identity anywhere in the head.** Title, hook, dek and
   primer all say "the account" / "an account". Neither the outfit nor what it
   did appears until §3's timeline. A follower count without an owner is a
   weaker hook than the same count with one, and it is the same defect that
   costs §2 its score.

**Aarav — yes.** "Vanish from your phone, and from nobody else's" is the
parallax move and he gets it cold. **Meera — yes, with a wince:** the hook, the
dek and the primer all open on the same seven words ("Twenty million followers
in a week" / "crossed twenty million followers in a week"). Three times on one
screen reads as repetition, not reinforcement. **Karthik — yes**, and he reads
"vanish" against the title's "hidden" and correctly takes the softer word as
the true one.

The gate's complaint about the missing standalone "you" is not a reader
finding. "Your phone" is the strongest "you" in the issue.

## The five things you asked me to test

### 1. Does a cold reader come away knowing *withheld* ≠ deleted? **Yes.**

All four get it, and **§1 does the work** — the `you-think` panel states the
wrong belief in the reader's own words ("A ban took the account down, so the
posts are gone and there is nothing left to open") and corrects it with a
figure and a picture in the same breath ("1 country … On a phone outside the
country every post is still there"). It is reinforced twice more, at §4
("Withheld is not deleted", the sharpest six words in the issue) and at §8's
"1" tile. Putting it first, before the reader has any investment, is what makes
it stick. **This is the draft's biggest success and it should not be touched.**

**The second half of the point is weaker.** That only the X account was
formally ordered, and that the website and Instagram losses are the founder's
account rather than a confirmed order, appears three times — §1's note, §3's
21 May annotation, §8's "1" tile note — and **all three are in note slots, in
compressed compound form** ("founder-reported" in one place, "founder-attributed"
in another, for the same idea). Karthik and Aarav get it. Meera gets it on the
tile. Sana skims past all three; she would tell a friend "they blocked his
whole setup". Not a BLOCK — the fact is in the draft three times and is never
misstated — but the direction is: say it once in a full sentence somewhere the
eye lands, and use one wording for it, not two.

**One conflation risk, and it sits in the primer.** "In May 2026 an account
crossed twenty million followers in a week. The government ordered X to hide
it from Indian users." Two adjacent sentences attach the follower figure to X.
The figure is an **Instagram** count (§5's caption and §8's tile 1 both say so)
while the order hit **X** (§1's note, §8's tile 2). The draft never writes the
sentence that joins them, so three of four readers merge the two platforms into
one number. Karthik noticed the mismatch and re-read. **The draft asserts
nothing false** — but it invites the reader to build the exact composite the
EDITOR NOTES exist to prevent. ⚠ worth the verifier's eye as well as the
stylist's.

### 2. Karthik and the statute material: **readable, with three unglossed objects.**

The skip test is trivially satisfied — **zero Hindi anywhere in the issue**, as
ruled. Nothing for him to lose. ("Janta" and "Janata" appear only inside two
proper names; "crore" is Indian English he counts in.) Using him on the law
instead:

**What reads:** §4's *withheld* gloss ("The post is still there. Your phone is
not allowed to fetch it.") is the best sentence in the draft for him — a
mechanism, not a definition. §7's three steps are clean end to end, and "The
secrecy is part of the law" is exactly the right register: it states the
statutory fact without a single legal word.

**Where it slides.** Three places name a legal object without giving it, and
each costs him a re-read:

- **"The 2021 rules everyone argues about are a separate track."** Defines 69A
  by contrast with an argument he has never been shown. He does not know there
  is a 2021 rulebook, who argues about it, or why it would be confused with
  this. The gloss spends a clause on ruling out a thing the reader never ruled
  in. (Related: the **2009 Blocking Rules**, which are the actual procedure and
  which §8c promised would survive, are nowhere in the draft. So the reader is
  told what the law *isn't* and never told what the procedure *is*.)
- **"Only a communication was."** Elliptical, and "a communication" is an
  object with no sender, no content and no size. He can infer "a letter from
  the ministry rather than the order itself", but he has to do it.
- **"The block now goes to the ministry's own review committee."** The word
  "own" is doing all the work and nothing else supports it. He reads it as "the
  body that wrote the order reviews the order" — which is almost certainly the
  point, and the draft never says it is the point, so it lands as procedure
  rather than as finding.

One dangler: **"The Supreme Court left that power standing in 2015."** Standing
against what? No challenge is described and no case is named (correctly — the
name was cut to save a slot). As written it is a half-fact.

### 3. Sana and the hook — see **The hook** above. Stops her; under-performs its own number.

### 4. Is the quote pair fair to all four? **Yes. Nobody comes away feeling an axe is being ground.**

The framing verb is "clarified", not "backtracked" or "claimed". The
clarification runs all 68 words with no ellipsis and no rebuttal after it. The
section title frames the remark as the origin of a name rather than as an
indictment, the eyebrow is "THE REMARK", and the epithet appears exactly once
in the whole issue, inside the quote, as the guardrail requires. No opposition
voice, no adjective, no editorial verb anywhere near it. **Aarav, Meera and
Karthik all read the pair as evidence rather than as argument. Sana — who is
the demographic the remark is about — reads it hot, but she reads the
*remark* hot, not the issue.** That is the correct outcome: the heat is in the
material and the page stays flat.

Two observations, neither a defect:

- **The draft never says there was a reaction.** The reader is not told the
  remark caused outrage, or that the clarification followed it. Aarav asked why
  the CJI clarified at all. The restraint is deliberate and it is the right
  call on this desk, but it does mean the pair sits slightly inert: the reader
  gets two texts and no reason the second one exists.
- **The clarification's own content ("fake and bogus degrees … they are like
  parasites") is left to the reader.** All four noticed the second epithet
  survives inside the retraction. None read the issue as pointing at it. Flat
  presentation is doing its job here.

### 5. The seams — one break, and one answer that arrives three sections late.

**The `number-sense` → `adoption-curve` jump lands well.** §6's intro opens
"Two crore followers in a week" — it picks up §5's own number in §5's own unit
and turns it into the next question ("where the order landed on that climb").
That is the restate-then-ask move working exactly as intended. Best seam in the
issue, alongside §7 → §8 ("That walk happens on paper you never see").

**The break is the seam *into* §5, not out of it.** §4 ends on three legal
terms; §5 opens "Twenty million is hard to picture." Nothing joins them. All
four readers registered the jolt; Meera, who leaves at the first paragraph that
does not pay off, is the one at risk here. The content is right and the door is
missing.

**`three-steps` at position 7 is late, but it should not be moved.** The
question "why couldn't the court see the order?" is raised twice early — §3's
29 May annotation ("The court was asked to weigh an order it never saw") and
§4's "Not on record" gloss — and answered only at §7 ("The secrecy is part of
the law", "argue against a document they are not allowed to read"). The reader
carries an open question across the two size sections. Moving §7 up would put
three text-only sections in a row and break the composition floors, so the fix
is a signal, not a move: §4's third gloss should tell the reader the reason is
coming, or §7's intro should name the question it is answering instead of only
restating the curve.

**Two smaller thread problems:**

- **The issue's own subject arrives in §3.** The head, §1 and §2 all say "the
  account" / "an account". The name, the pun and the word "satirical" all land
  in §3. §2 is titled *Where the name came from* and does not contain the name
  — the single clearest comprehension defect in the draft, and the reason this
  is a REVISE.
- **"Its founder was in Boston." (§3, 16 May note)** reads as a stray fact to
  all four. It is in fact load-bearing — the person the order is aimed at is
  outside the jurisdiction, which is the hero's argument in one detail — and
  nothing on the page makes the connection. Either connect it or cut it.

Ordered by what it buys per word.

8. **The founder-reported caveat is said twice in two different compressions**

9. **Two facts are placed without their reason:** "Its founder was in Boston"

10. **§9, the hero's fourth row.** "Reach the saved posts", filled `full` for
