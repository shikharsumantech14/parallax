## C-01 · The Bill Came Due in April

- **status:** chosen
- **why now:** On 2 June 2026, Bloomberg reported (relayed by Simon Willison
  on 3 June) that Uber now caps every employee at **$1,500/month in token
  spend per AI coding tool** after it **exhausted its entire 2026 AI budget
  in four months**. Willison notes the cap alone is ~11% of a median Uber
  engineer's $330K comp. It lands the same fortnight as his PyCon talk dating
  the coding-agent "daily-driver" crossover to a November 2025 inflection.
- **angle:** Everyone reads "AI is getting cheaper" — per-token prices keep
  falling. Parallax inverts it: the *bill* is exploding anyway. An agentic
  coding session burns 5–30x the tokens of a chat turn, and reasoning models
  inflate token-per-task faster than price-per-token drops. The structural
  truth is a scissor: a falling unit price multiplied by a faster-rising unit
  count equals a budget blown in a third of a year. Uber's cap is the first
  visible institution hitting the wall everyone else is about to.
- **suggested kinds:** hero, paradox (price-per-token down / spend-per-task
  up — the load-bearing reframe), timeline (Nov-2025 coding inflection →
  budget set in 2025 → blown by Apr 2026 → June cap), scaling-plot
  (tokens-per-task vs task complexity, log axis — needs the 5k→1M/task range),
  benchmark-chart (cost per agentic session across tools/models, $ bars),
  prose, data-readout (the $1,500 cap vs $330K comp ratio as a single readout)
- **est. read time:** 7 min
- **sources (3-5):**

  - https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/

  inflection) are all on Willison (allowlisted). The richer unit-economics
