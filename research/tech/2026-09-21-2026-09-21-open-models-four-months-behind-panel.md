## The hook

**"Kimi K3 took 84 days to reach the frontier OpenAI had in April. You can download it free. Almost nobody has."**

- **Aarav:** Partial yes. The "free… almost nobody has" turn is a real hook, but "Kimi K3" and "the frontier OpenAI had in April" arrive with no gloss in the hook itself, so the first clause is inert until section 1 catches him up.
- **Meera:** Yes. The concrete number, the direct "you," and the twist ("almost nobody has") are exactly the shape that keeps her past paragraph one.
- **Sana:** Partial. "Free… nobody has" is intriguing, but "84 days" is not converted into something she can feel (e.g. "under three months") and "Kimi K3" means nothing to her yet — it stops her thumb on curiosity, not on recognition.
- **Karthik:** Yes. No Hindi, specific and falsifiable-sounding, reads clean to an engineer.

**It lacked:** a felt-scale conversion for "84 days" (weeks? "under three months"?) for Sana, and one word of orientation on what "the frontier" means before the hook spends its first clause on an unfamiliar model name.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 4 (benchmark-chart):** the intro states the headline gap in months ("four months is an average") while the chart itself is drawn in points, and the four sampled bars (58, 33, 25, 3) don't average to the chart's own "eight points" title — because they're deliberately the extremes, not a representative sample. Nothing in the section says so. Give the reader one clause that tells them these four are the widest and narrowest, not an average sample, so an analytical reader doesn't stop to check arithmetic that was never meant to reconcile.
2. **Section 6 (arch-stack) intro:** "kharcha" is the head noun of its clause and has no English synonym in the same sentence — delete it and the sentence loses its object, not just its warmth. Karthik's skip test fails here specifically (though the section's data itself carries him through). Restructure so the sentence stands on its own English noun, with the Hindi riding alongside it rather than carrying it.
3. **Section 8 (prose, the closer):** it opens on "So the open model costs less than a third as much," which only resolves if the reader is still holding section 7's ₹4,795/₹1,439 numbers — and because this is the last section, there is no following intro to restate its finding for a reader who loses that thread. Give the opening sentence its own footing, and land the "why" (the four months is cheap; the rest of the stack is what people actually can't afford) as an explicit closing line rather than leaving readers to infer it from section 6.
4. **Section 5 (version-graph):** for a reader who doesn't read fork/lineage diagrams natively (Sana), the intro moves straight from "those gaps run three to fifty-eight" to naming three unfamiliar labs. One more clause of orientation — what a lane and a joining line mean — before the names arrive would give her a foothold the plain line alone doesn't provide at a scroll's glance.
