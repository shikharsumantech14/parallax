## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

The reader arrives thinking 2024 YR4 was a scare that scientists kept
walking back: a "4% chance" headline that turned into "never mind." The
structural truth is the opposite of indecision. The impact probability rose
and then collapsed to zero because the *uncertainty* collapsed, not because
the rock moved. Right after discovery, the asteroid's position on
22 December 2032 was known only as a long, fat error ellipse — a cone of
possible places it could be. The Moon sat inside that cone, so the odds of
hitting it were a few percent. Every new observation was another set of
photons that narrowed the cone; each pass made the asteroid's future
position more certain, and as the cone shrank the Moon eventually fell
*outside* it and the odds snapped to zero. Crucially, the number went *up*
before it went down: the Earth-impact figure peaked at 3.1% on 18 February
2025 — the highest NASA has ever recorded for an object this size — then
fell to 0.004% within a week, and the risk "migrated" to the Moon (peaking
near 4%) before vanishing too. A probability that climbs to a peak and then
craters is not a system failing; it is the exact, expected signature of a
planetary-defense system *working* — refining a position faster than the
public can refine its panic. The revelation: certainty here was manufactured
on a schedule, one telescope pass at a time, and the scariest moment (the
peak) was the moment the system had the least light, not the most danger.

## 2. Why now

On **5 March 2026**, NASA's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies and ESA's
Near-Earth Object Coordination Centre jointly announced that asteroid
**2024 YR4 will not impact the Moon** on 22 December 2032. The conclusion
rested on two James Webb Space Telescope (NIRCam) observations made on
**18 and 26 February 2026** — among the faintest observations of an asteroid
ever made — which pinned the orbit down two years before any ground-based
telescope could have (the asteroid is otherwise unobservable until its 2028
return). The 2026 observations dropped the lunar-impact odds from a standing
**4.3%** to nil; the rock will instead pass **more than 20,000 km** beyond
the Moon (NASA: 13,200 miles / 21,200 km above the lunar surface). This
closes a ~15-month risk-resolution arc that opened with the December 2024
discovery, ran through the record Earth-impact peak in February 2025, and
migrated to the Moon through 2025.
