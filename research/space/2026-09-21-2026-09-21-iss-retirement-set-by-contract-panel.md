## The hook

*"You think cracks and leaks are ending the space station. The date came from a contract NASA signed in 2024, six years early."*

- **Aarav:** Yes. The reversal (cracks → contract) is exactly his register; "six years early" is a good twist word.
- **Meera:** Yes. Enough of a claim to keep scrolling past the dek and primer to the first graphic.
- **Sana:** Borderline yes — the "you think X" reversal works, but it lacks a number or image she can *feel* (no rupee figure, no size, nothing to picture). It stops the thumb on the reversal alone, not on a concrete thing.
- **Karthik:** Yes. Plain English throughout, no barrier.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 2's opening sentence has an unclear referent right after the hook** — "That falling line was drawn years before it will be flown" — the "it" that "will be flown" doesn't clearly point at anything a reader has met yet. This is the highest-cost fix: it sits exactly where Meera decides whether to keep going.
2. **Q1's actual "why 2030" is never stated.** The draft teaches "a contract's delivery date decides the year," which is true and lands well, but it never says the five partner agencies committed to operate the station only through 2030 in the first place (the timeline's "Russia's commitment runs only to 2028" note implies it but doesn't say it for the others). One clause — in the timeline's caption or an annotation on the 2030 row — would close this for all four readers.
3. **Q2 teaches only one of its three drivers.** The three-steps card nails "the rocket takes years to buy" but never mentions a replacement station needing certification or extra cargo/crew flights being bought years ahead — both are elsewhere in the dossier (§4.4) but not in the draft. Either add a line naming one more driver, or treat the question as answered by the lead-time argument alone.
4. **Row 3's timeline is dense for a scanning reader.** Six dated events land as a wall for Sana; she keeps the headline date (2027) and loses the causal chain between the 2026 audit and the 2029/2030 dates. Consider whether the annotation or caption can carry the chain more directly, so the sequence doesn't depend on holding six dates in working memory.
5. **Row 8's "aasmaan" costs Karthik a sentence**, even though the caption's numbers rescue the fact. The English half of that sentence ("Same strip of ___, one arriving as the other leaves") needs to carry the same meaning on its own, per the skip test.
