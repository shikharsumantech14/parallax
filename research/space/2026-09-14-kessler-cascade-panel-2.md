## Findings on the eight points

**1 · Section 5's `followup`. Yes, Sana moves, 3 → 4, and the section goes 16 → 19.**
It now does the job rule 5 gives it: the quote's first two clauses restated in
words Sana uses, then a forward pointer. Her retell above is the evidence — she
reproduces the mechanism without reproducing the agency's vocabulary, which is
exactly what the old field failed to buy her. "Floor" is gone from the issue and
nothing competes with the altitude frame. Two things worth recording. First,
"What comes next is the bill" is picked up three sections later by section 8's
eyebrow, "THE SHARED BILL", so the last three sections now read as one act
instead of three readings. Second, the `followup` translates two of the quote's
three clauses. The third, that removal is required, lands at section 7's zero
tile, which is where every reader repeats it from anyway. No question depends on
it. Not a defect.

**2 · Section 1's drag. Yes, both blinks are gone.**
Aarav and Sana both read the mechanism as one move now: the air pulls down,
break-ups add faster. The gain is bigger than the fix, because section 3's
"Decay" gloss, which repeats "thin air up there" almost word for word, now
reads to both of them as confirmation of something they were already told
rather than as a fact arriving late. That is why section 3 holds at 20 and
section 1 rose. The one residual is small and does not hold anything back:
"break-ups add them faster than it can" asks Sana to carry "it" back past a
comma to "thin air", with the verb elided. One beat, and she still retells it
correctly.

**3 · The gloss at the second use. It works where it sits, and it cannot reach backwards.**
Glossing at section 7 is the right call and it bought the thing that mattered:
the tile is no longer a *second* unglossed use, so Meera and Sana do not
compound. Meera says so in her section 7 retell. It does nothing for the
section 2 annotation, because a linear reader meets the annotation five sections
earlier — but the annotation was never costing more than one re-read, and it
still costs exactly that. Separate the two problems: **the term is not what
stops Meera.** "Near miss" is transparent Indian English and Aarav, Sana and
Karthik all convert it to "close calls" and move on. What stops her is "a tenth
of a risk" — a share whose base is never given, at either site. The gloss
defines the noun, not the quantity, and at 12 words the annotation could never
have done both. So: the placement was right, and the residual is a different
defect wearing the same phrase.

**4 · The paradox. Yes, it reads as two forces in genuine tension.**
Karthik, who found the mismatch in the first pass, now says the statement and
its evidence agree, and he is right: a non-binding resolution evidences "nobody
is bound" exactly. All four retells above carry the tension in the same shape —
the cost falls on whoever is still flying, the obligation falls on nobody. The
"owes" label bridges pay and bound, so the label no longer contradicts its own
statement. One seam survives and it is smaller than the one it replaced:
**the section title still carries "pays" on both halves while side B now carries
"bound"**, so Meera's noticing moved from statement-versus-evidence to
title-versus-statement. The intro funds the money half in the same breath
("only one operator has ever been fined"), so she resolves it inside the
section. My recommendation is to leave the title alone — all four retold the
section from it in the first pass, it is the most repeatable six words in the
issue, and any change would have to be word-for-word inside its own length.

**5 · The zero as a full sentence. Confirmed, and it does what it was meant to.**
"Nothing has ever been removed from orbit, and only one operator has ever been
fined" is now the first thing the reader meets in the closer. It reaches back to
the zero tile without restructuring, and it funds side B's money half. It does
not move the issue's peak — the fact three of four readers still say they would
repeat is the zero tile itself in section 7, not the closer. The closer is now
warmer for it, and that was the available gain.

**6 · India's abstention. The blur is closed.**
"India abstained, neither for nor against, and the resolution binds nobody."
Four words of meaning attached to "abstained", exactly the move the first pass
asked for, and no reader can now blur India into the nine. The sentence still
ends on the instrument rather than on the country, which is what keeps the
section fair. Sana's retell is the test: "India did not vote either way", stated
flat, with no charge in it.

**7 · The missing total. The issue does not read as incomplete. Worth a research job, not a gate.**
Judged fresh, without the first report. No retell fails for want of it, no quiz
answer needs it, and the two facts readers carry away — the altitude split and
the zero — are both untouched by it. The issue never promises a level: it
promises a direction and a price, and it delivers both. Three readers still
reach for a stock figure, and the reach is cheap to trace: section 1 says "the
total still climbs" and the caption says "the debris total still rises", so the
word "total" is used twice before anything sizes it. What they find instead is
7,473 objects added in 2024, which is a flow read as a stock. That is one
unanswered curiosity, not a hole in the argument.

**My call: schedule it, do not block on it.** One published catalogued-object

1's `note`, it costs about a dozen words of the 131 spare and no name — but it
needs a source read, which this rewrite may not do. It is worth a research job
the next time the space desk opens src-01, and it is worth nothing if it
arrives as a number nobody published.

**8 · The dek. Standing by it, unchanged.**
Comprehension cost is one re-read for Meera on the noun-less "tests", and no
