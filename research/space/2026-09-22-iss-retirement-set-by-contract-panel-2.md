## The hook

*"You think cracks and leaks are ending the space station. The date came from a contract NASA signed in 2024, six years early."*

- **Aarav:** Yes. The reversal plus a specific date lands exactly in his register.
- **Meera:** Borderline yes. Pays off by the primer, but the hook itself carries no number she can feel — it names an institutional fact (a contract, a year), not a stake she owns.
- **Sana:** No. Nothing to picture, no rupee or lakh figure, no person. "Contract" and "2024" don't compete well against her feed. It needed a concrete thing to picture, not a procurement fact.
- **Karthik:** Yes. The specificity ("signed... six years early") reads as a clean anomaly to an engineer's ear.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 4's closing step now carries the right content but the wrong shape.** "Count back from 2030, then add certifying a replacement station and buying extra flights: 2027" tries to do three jobs in one clause — a subtraction, an addition, and a landing number — and the numbers do not visibly add up to the number given, which is exactly what an attentive reader (Karthik) notices and what a scanning reader (Sana) simply lets blur. Split it: one sentence for "the rocket's own lead time already points to around 2027," a second, separate sentence for "and NASA still has to certify a replacement and lock in extra flights before then" — two claims that both support 2027 rather than one that appears to stack past it.
2. **Section 7 is still the thinnest retell in the issue, unchanged from the previous read.** Two abstract arguments (the GAO's missing test, Hoeser's legal-authority claim) share one prose block, and the opening "calendars nearly touch" metaphor pays off only in section 8. Either split the two arguments into their own sentences with a connective between them, or move one clause of section 8's altitude-overlap fact earlier so the metaphor can cash out where it's introduced.
3. **Section 1 still never says the date itself hasn't been picked** — only that time is "counted back from the final burn, not calendar dates." The storyboard's own model answer for Q1 is built on "a day nobody has picked yet"; one clause in the `plain` or `caption` making that explicit would close the gap Sana's retell (and the storyboard's own framing) keeps landing on.
