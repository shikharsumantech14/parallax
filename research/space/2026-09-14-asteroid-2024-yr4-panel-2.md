## The seven stylist changes, judged

**1 · Jun 3's "the Moon's share of that smaller space rises" — worked. Sana is
whole on Q2.** She can now say *why*, not just *that*: the note carries a causal
"so" between a shrinking range and a rising share, and the Feb 24 note two
events earlier supplies what the share is a share of. One residual, small: the
word *share* does two jobs in one phrase — the odds are split across the space,
and the Moon's fraction *of the space* grows. The reader lands on the right
picture either way. If this note is ever re-cut, the thing to protect is the
"so", not the wording.

*Direction:* the Jun 3 **annotation** still says only "The risk moves to the
Moon. The rock does not." — the *what*, not the *why* — and an annotation on the
mark is the most-read text in a timeline. The *why* now lives in the note below
it. That asymmetry is the last thing standing between this section and a 5.

**2 · Feb 24's denominator — worked, and it arrives at the right event.** Aarav
gets Q2 defensibly for the first time. It costs him one re-read because the
abstraction ("whatever is still inside the range of possible positions") precedes
its concrete name by a section. Acceptable: the name arrives in §03 and the
sentence is comprehensible without it.

**3 · The `jargon-buster` reorder — worked, and it did more than fix the order.
Keep three terms.** With "The error box" leading, the concrete object defines the
abstraction instead of following it, and term 2 can now point back at "that box".
Aarav does not drop term 1 any more: he keeps all three and retells them in
order. More importantly, as above, the reorder made this section the skimmer's
route to Q2 — dropping to two terms would cost that, and term 3 (the 1% line) is
the only thing in the issue that answers "is 4% a lot".

The section's honest cost is Meera's: terms 1 and 2 now restate what the timeline
just taught, so the only *new* information here is term 3. That is a legitimate
restatement (contract rule 5) and a 4, not a defect — but see the repetition
finding below.

**4 · The almond's described speaker — worked. It reads as sourced.** "The
astronomers who led those runs said it reflects…" gives the quoted fragment a
human subject and a verb, which is what the quote marks were promising and not
delivering. The §8c.4 ruling has now shipped in both halves (taken, and described
rather than named). **Webb and Gaia both carry roles now** — "Webb, a space
telescope" is complete; "the Gaia mission's star map" gives Gaia a name and a
product but never says why star positions are what you measure an asteroid
against. Karthik is the only reader who asks, and no question depends on it.

**5 · Tile 5's citation and tile 3's ESA — one worked, one did not.** Tile 3 is
clean: "Europe's space agency later called it around 4%" is plain, self-glossing,
and the acronym survives on the section source line where rule 9 wants it. Tile 5
lost its citation and kept the problem: two figures for one distance, in two unit
systems, with no sentence saying why there are two. The first report asked for
one distance and one unit; what shipped removed the agency names only. This is
the single first-pass finding that did not land.

**6 · The closer's gloss of "improved precision" — worked, and it is the
cleanest fix of the seven.** The hard half of the verbatim quote is now opened in
the next breath, in the *same words the timeline used* ("the range of possible
positions kept shrinking"). That phrase now appears three times across the issue
in three registers, which is what makes it stick. Aarav takes the landing with
the quote understood rather than skipped. Karthik confirms the skip test:
delete *aasmaan* and "The rock is still up there, on the same path it has always
flown" says everything.

**7 · "Two years before any ground telescope could have" — worked, and it closes
the one storyboard-to-draft shortfall the first read found.** Q3 is now answerable
in full. One structural note: this clause answers *what Webb bought* (two years),
while the reason *why the ground could not* (the faintness, the almond) sits in
the previous node — the 34-word one that Meera and Sana both abandon. So the
consequence is in the note everyone reads and the cause is in the note they skip.
That is the right way round if only one of them can be read, and no question
depends on the cause.

## Point 9 — section 5's title, "Only one of six belongs to the rock"

**The patch is enough.** The intro now sorts all six figures into three kinds:
the odds describe how well we knew the orbit, the size belongs to the rock, the
date and the distance belong to the close approach. It renders above the tiles,
so the two readers who count (Meera, Karthik) meet the sort before they meet the
figures, and both accept the title.

Two residuals, neither worth reopening:

- The title states a two-way split and the intro has to qualify it into a
  three-way one, so the title is a promise the intro pays. A reader who reads
  only the titles — rule 11's test — gets "only one of six belongs to the rock",

  readings of *us*, not of the rock.

  is something the rock does. He accepts the intro's separation without stalling.

No quiz question depends on it, no reader stalls, and the patch cost 38 words
that also do restatement work for §04. Leave it.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

Ordered by what it buys per word. **None of these holds the verdict** — the issue
teaches its argument as it stands.

1. **§05, tile 5: one distance, one unit.** The only over-cap field that costs
   the reader. Pick the figure the reader should carry and let the source line

   two, and that costs more words than the tile has.
2. **§02, Feb 2026: pay the trim out of the Gaia clause.** The almond and the
   speaker phrase are what the extra words bought. The star-map plumbing is what
   they should have come out of.
3. **§04, the `note`: a quotation with no speaker.** Quote marks announce that
   somebody said this and then decline to say who, in the section whose whole job
   is to make one number sayable. Either give it the speaker the almond just got,
   or let it be the issue's own sentence with the source line carrying it.
4. **§03, term 1: give back the echo, not the gloss.** The duplicated clause is
   the trim; the definition sentence is untouchable.
5. **§02, Jun 3: the annotation carries the *what*, the note carries the *why*.**
   The annotation is the more-read of the two. If the callout is ever re-cut,
   the rise is what belongs on the mark.
