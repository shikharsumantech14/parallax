# Dossier: More than half of India's "international arrivals" are Indians coming home

- **Category:** travel
- **Candidate:** C-01
- **Researched:** 2026-09-17
- **Researcher:** researcher-agent
- **Status:** ready-for-draft

---

## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

You read that India got **20.22 million international tourist arrivals** in 2025 and
you picture 20 million foreigners. **11.07 million of them were Indians** — people
with Indian passports or Indian families living abroad, flying home. Only **9.15
million** were foreign tourists, and that number is **16.3% below 2019**, six years
after the pandemic ended. The gap is not a rounding footnote. It is a counting
convention: India's headline "ITA" figure is defined as foreign tourist arrivals
**plus** arrivals of Non-Resident Indians, and the two halves are moving in opposite
directions — the foreign half fell 8.07% in 2025 while the NRI half rose 4.26%. The
money tells you which half is which. Total arrivals slipped only 1.71%, but
foreign-exchange earnings from tourism fell **5.53% in rupees** (₹2,93,033 crore →
₹2,76,831 crore), because the growing half sleeps in a relative's spare room and the
shrinking half books a hotel. The UN's own statistical manual predicted exactly this:
nationals residing abroad "mainly stay with family and friends" rather than visiting
tourist sites, which is why it tells countries to count them separately. India does
count them separately. It just reports them added together — and then reads the sum
as a recovery.

---

## 2. Why now

- **The 2025 full-year numbers are on the board.** The Ministry of Tourism's India
  Tourism Data Portal dashboard shows calendar 2025 closing at **20.22 million ITAs
  (−1.71%)**, of which **9.15 million FTAs (−8.07%)** and **11.07 million NRI arrivals
  (+4.26%)**. This is the first full year in which the NRI half exceeds the foreign
  half by more than 1.9 million, and the second consecutive year of a falling foreign
  half in a "recovered" travel world.
- **The ministry itself said the quiet part out loud on 14 September 2026.** Suman
  Billa, Additional Secretary at the Ministry of Tourism, told Skift that outbound is
  growing at 4.8%, faster than inbound, and warned against the "smugness" of leaning
  on the domestic market. Domestic tourist visits hit **4,287 million in 2025 (+45.6%)**
  — 468 domestic visits for every one foreign tourist.
- **The Bangladesh shock is now a full year old and fully measurable.** Bangladesh,
  India's **second-largest source market in 2024** at 17,50,165 arrivals (17.59% of
  all FTAs), fell to **466,012 in 2025 — a 73.37% collapse** after India's August 2024
  visa restrictions. Strip Bangladesh out and the rest of the foreign half actually
  *grew* 4.25%. One border decision and one counting convention are doing all the
  work in a headline that reads as neither.

## 4. Key facts & data

### 4a. The headline identity — ITA = FTA + NRI

This is the load-bearing fact of the whole issue. The Ministry of Tourism's own
dashboard publishes all three series, and they reconcile exactly.

- **2025 ITA:** 20.22 million, **−1.71%** on 2024 — [Ministry of Tourism data portal](https://data.tourism.gov.in/dashboard)
- **2025 FTA (foreign tourists):** 9.15 million, **−8.07%** — [same](https://data.tourism.gov.in/dashboard)
- **2025 NRI arrivals:** 11.07 million, **+4.26%** — [same](https://data.tourism.gov.in/dashboard)
- **Arithmetic check (verified by researcher):** 9.15 + 11.07 = 20.22 ✓
- **NRI share of 2025 ITAs:** 11.07 ÷ 20.22 = **54.75%** → **55 in every 100**
- **FTA share of 2025 ITAs:** **45.25%** → **45 in every 100**
- **2019 identity check:** 17.91 ITA − 10.93 FTA = 6.98 NRI, matching the printed NRI figure ✓
- **2024 identity check:** 9.95 + 10.62 = 20.57 ✓
- **FTA 2025 vs 2019:** 9.15 vs 10.93 = **−16.29%** (matches the "16.3% below 2019" reported by Skift, 31 Jul 2026)
