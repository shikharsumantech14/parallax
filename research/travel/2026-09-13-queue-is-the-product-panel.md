## The hook

> "Everest ka ticket: $15,000, about ₹12.5 lakh. Fuji ka ticket: ₹2,300. Both
> countries are now selling the same thing — your place in the line."

Stopped the thumb for all four. The ₹12.5 lakh against ₹2,300 is the whole
engine: two prices for the same act, a gap you can feel without doing any
arithmetic, and a twist in the last clause. Aarav, Meera and Sana were in
without a pause. Karthik read "ka" twice and carried on — a beat lost, not a
meaning.

It lacked nothing for attention. It has one comprehension cost, and it is the
issue's own most exposed claim: **"selling… your place in the line" reads as
*the money buys you a better position*.** That is precisely what the issue
later says is not true, and the correction ("no money buys a place at the
front") is 13.5px grey type five sections away. Sana left with the hook's
reading, which is why her Q2 came back half-right.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 4: the intro calls the three cards "three moves" both governments
   made — cards 1 and 2 are conditions, not moves.** Name what the three
   actually are (a chain that ends in a decision), so the reader is not
   reconciling two framings while reading the cards.
2. **Section 7: "Three seasons" contradicts a rail that starts in 2013.**
   Either the intro's span matches the six dates on the graphic, or the 2013
   and 2019 nodes are named as the before-picture the three seasons answer.
3. **Every section's restatement is in `skimCaption`, which does not render in
   Full mode.** Six of the eight carry the one line the reader most needs
   ("About ₹12.5 lakh buys entry to the Everest line, not a place at the
   front"). The sentence the reader actually meets after a graphic is the next
   section's intro — put the restatement there, or in the caption.

Also, in descending cost:

4. **The hook promises "your place in the line"; the issue's correction is a
   13.5px note.** Raise "no money buys a place at the front" to copy the reader
   cannot skip — an intro or a caption — in section 1 or 2. This is the
   storyboard's own §8d.1 standing constraint, and right now the loudest
   sentence in the issue pulls against it.
5. **"4,000 a day" has no comparison anywhere in the issue.** A cap with no
   before-number cannot be felt as a cap; Meera and Karthik both asked "out of
   how many?" and the draft never answers. (The dossier's climber range was
   dropped on purpose — so the comparison has to be a habit, not a figure.)
6. **Section 6: four of seven rows are un-glossed shorthand** — "Guide 1:2",
   "55-day permit window", "competence screen", "5th station", and the
   *(enacted)* / *(proposed)* tags. Gloss the ratio where it appears; the tags
   need one line somewhere saying what the two words are doing.
7. **Section 1: the Nepali-rupee note sits beside a ₹ figure with no relation
   stated**, so all four readers converted it in their heads and three of them
   would get it wrong. State the relation in words; the storyboard's ruling 8
   bars the rate, not the sentence.
8. **Section 8: the old-deal panel describes the old deal using the new
   rule** — climbers "pushing up Fuji overnight to beat the gate", in the era
   before the gate. And the Everest half of "pay more, stay less" is asserted,
   never shown: nothing in the draft says the $15,000 shortens anyone's wait.
9. **Section 5's caption ("Mount Fuji · Yoshida Trail · 2026 season") and
   section 8's ("What changes when the queue becomes the thing for sale") are
   labels, not claims** — the reader looks at the caption for the finding and
   gets a filing card. Form-vs-claim is the verifier's flag; the comprehension
   cost is that two sections restate nothing at all.
