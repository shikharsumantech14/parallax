## The hook

**"You read 20 million international arrivals in 2025. Only 9.15 million were foreigners. The other 11.07 million were Indians flying home."**

- **Aarav** — yes. A number he half-remembers from a headline, immediately contradicted.
- **Meera** — yes, on the first line: the gap between "20 million" and "9.15 million" is exactly the kind of thing she'd screenshot in a cab.
- **Sana** — yes, but softer. It has the number and the twist; it does not have a "you" that is personally hers — she has not read a 20-million-arrivals headline, so the opening premise ("You read...") is borrowed. The twist still lands once she gets past the first clause.
- **Karthik** — yes. Clean, numeric, no Hindi to parse, states its own contradiction.

## What would fix it (direction only, never the sentence)

1. **Section 3 (timeline):** the caption's "6.98 million" for 2019 is not derivable from anything the reader has actually seen — the 2019 event gives only the foreign count and a ratio. State the Indian figure (or the ratio, "39 in every 100") inside the 2019 event note itself, so the caption restates data the graphic showed rather than introducing new arithmetic.
2. **Section 6 (prose):** the ₹2,93,033 crore → ₹2,76,831 crore fall has no scale a reader can feel, and handing over the 9.51% dollar figure right after establishing 5.53% in rupees risks the wrong number sticking. Give the rupee fall (₹16,202 crore) a comparison the reader owns, and make explicit — in the same sentence, not by implication — that 5.53% is the one to remember and 9.51% is the misleading headline.
3. **Section 5 (attrition-waffle):** the denominator ambiguity is the honest and correct disclosure, but it arrives only in the caption's last sentence, after the intro and title have already let the reader assume a specific population (100 what?). Move the caveat — or at least a flag that one exists — into the intro or the plain line, before the numbers are read, not after.
4. **The primer:** "Foreign tourists fell 8% last year. Indians coming home rose 4%." — neither percentage appears anywhere in the sections below (the timeline gives only raw counts for 2024→2025). Either state these two percentages where the 2025 timeline event already sits, or don't promise a number the reader can never go and check.
