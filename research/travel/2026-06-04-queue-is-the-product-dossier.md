## 1. Structural argument (the Parallax take)

You think the news at Everest and Fuji is "overtourism rules are tightening" —
governments making two crowded mountains a little harder to reach. The
structural truth is sharper: at both peaks the scarce good being priced is no
longer the summit, it is **the bottleneck** — the queue, the slot, the window
of safe weather that everyone competes for at once. Nepal did not cap Everest;
it re-priced it, raising the base spring royalty from $11,000 to $15,000 and
stacking qualifying conditions on top — a mandatory guide ratio now, and a
slate of proposed gates (a Nepal-7,000-metre prerequisite, Nepali-only guides,
death-body-recovery insurance) that screen who is even allowed to join the
line. Japan went further and turned the crowd itself into inventory: the
Yoshida Trail up a UNESCO-listed sacred volcano is now a hard ceiling of
**4,000 climbers a day**, each paying **¥4,000**, most of them holding a
**reserved, timed entry slot booked online** like a concert ticket, with the
gate slammed shut at 2 p.m. to kill the overnight "bullet climb." The mechanism
that creates the gap between the surface story and the structure: on a mountain,
the dangerous resource was never the rock — it was *time spent waiting in the
death zone* (Everest) or *bodies arriving in the same hours* (Fuji). Once an
authority can meter that — by price, by quota, by a clock on a gate — the
queue stops being a failure of management and becomes the thing that is bought
and sold. The reader ends understanding that "managing the crowd" and
"selling the crowd" are now the same administrative act.

## 2. Why now

Two enacted 2026-season changes converge:

- **Nepal / Everest.** On **1 September 2025** Nepal began enforcing a higher
  Everest royalty — the spring (March–May) south-route fee rose from **$11,000
  to $15,000** per foreign climber, the first revision since 2015. It is the
  sixth amendment to the Mountaineering (Expedition) Regulation 2002, published
  in the **Nepal Gazette on 3 February 2025** and approved by Cabinet on
  **8 January 2025**. The 2026 spring season is the first full Everest season
  climbed under the new fee.
  [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect)
  · [Alan Arnette](https://www.alanarnette.com/blog/2025/09/03/everest-just-became-more-expensive-and-unattractive-to-some/)
- **Japan / Fuji.** For the **2026 season (1 July – 10 September 2026)** the
  Yoshida Trail keeps its **4,000-climbers-per-day cap**, the **¥4,000**
  entry fee, the online entry-slot reservation, and the **14:00 gate closure**
  to end overnight "bullet climbing." Yamanashi Prefecture's official notice
  for the 2026 season was posted **13 March 2026**; secondary coverage dates
  the opening of online reservations to **27 April 2026** ([UNVERIFIED] against
  the official page — see §4/§9).
  [Yamanashi Prefecture / fujisan-climb.jp](https://www.fujisan-climb.jp/en/yamanashi-notice/)

This becomes the hero "why now" and the final node of the timeline.

## 4. Key facts & data

### VERIFIED — safe for the verified spine

These are sourced to a primary government source, the national paper of record

**Everest / Nepal**

- **Spring Everest royalty (foreign, south route):** raised from **$11,000 →
  $15,000** per climber, effective **1 September 2025**; first revision since
  2015. — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect) (quoting Dept. of Tourism Director Himal Gautam) · [Alan Arnette](https://www.alanarnette.com/blog/2025/09/03/everest-just-became-more-expensive-and-unattractive-to-some/)
- **Full Nepal royalty table (old → new, per person):** Autumn **$5,500 →
  $7,500**; Winter & Monsoon **$2,750 → $3,750**; Nepali citizens (spring)
  **Rs 75,000 → Rs 150,000**; other 8,000 m peaks (spring) **$1,800 → $3,000**.
  — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect) · [Alan Arnette](https://www.alanarnette.com/blog/2025/09/03/everest-just-became-more-expensive-and-unattractive-to-some/)
- **Permit validity:** cut from **75 days → 55 days**. — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect)
- **Mandatory guide ratio (enacted):** **one guide/Sherpa per two climbers**
  on peaks above 8,000 m (was effectively 1:1); one guide per 15 climbers on
  lower peaks. Widely described as under-enforced. — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect) · [Alan Arnette](https://www.alanarnette.com/blog/2025/09/03/everest-just-became-more-expensive-and-unattractive-to-some/)
- **Legal instrument:** 6th amendment to the Mountaineering (Expedition)
  Regulation 2002; Cabinet **8 Jan 2025**; Nepal Gazette **3 Feb 2025**;
  enforced **1 Sep 2025**. — [Syracuse JILC](https://jilc.syr.edu/2025/10/22/saving-everest-nepals-new-attempt-to-curb-years-of-issues-on-the-worlds-highest-peak/) · [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/09/02/new-everest-permit-fee-of-15-000-takes-effect)
- **2025 season permit volume:** **374 climbers from 49 countries**,
  generating **~$4 million** in royalties (vs **359 people / $2.48M** in 2015).
  — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/mount-everest-new-rules-tours-nepal)
- **Recent death tolls:** **2019: 11 deaths** (Arnette: ~5 crowd-related);
  **2023: 18**; **2024: 8**. — [National Geographic (crowding)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/everest-season-deaths-controversy-crowding-perpetual-planet) · [National Geographic (rules)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/mount-everest-new-rules-tours-nepal)
- **Body-recovery cost (the figure the proposed insurance would price):**
  **$30,000–$70,000 per body.** — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/mount-everest-new-rules-tours-nepal)
- **Guiding-service cost (context for the $15k royalty):** climbers already pay
  **$60,000–$100,000** for guiding; NatGeo also cites total cost "over £80,000."
  — [Syracuse JILC](https://jilc.syr.edu/2025/10/22/saving-everest-nepals-new-attempt-to-curb-years-of-issues-on-the-worlds-highest-peak/) · [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/how-to-climb-mount-everest-himalayas-responsibly)
- **China-side contrast:** China minimizes crowding by issuing far fewer
  permits; some outfitters have moved to the China side. — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/everest-season-deaths-controversy-crowding-perpetual-planet)

**Everest South Col route — elevation profile (for `elevation-trek`)**

- Base Camp **5,364 m** → Camp 1 **6,065 m** → Camp 2 **6,400 m** → Camp 3
  **7,162 m** → Camp 4 / South Col **7,950 m** → Summit **8,848.86 m**. The
  **"death zone" begins at the South Col (~7,950 m / ~26,000 ft)**; climbers
  have ~3 days max to make a summit bid from there. The summit-ridge
  **bottleneck** sits between the South Col and the top (the Hillary Step /
  Balcony zone, ~28,000 ft). — [Alan Arnette, South Col route maps](https://www.alanarnette.com/everest/everestsouthroutes.php) · [South Col, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Col) (cross-check only) · death-zone altitude corroborated by [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/everest-season-deaths-controversy-crowding-perpetual-planet)

  — [fujisan-climb.jp official notice](https://www.fujisan-climb.jp/en/yamanashi-notice/)

- **Issuing authority:** **Yamanashi Prefecture, Tourism, Culture and Sports
  Department, Mt. Fuji Tourism Promotion Group.** — [fujisan-climb.jp official notice](https://www.fujisan-climb.jp/en/yamanashi-notice/)
- **Equipment screening at the 5th-station gate:** climbers must have winter
  clothing, two-piece rain gear, and proper footwear to pass. — [fujisan-climb.jp official notice](https://www.fujisan-climb.jp/en/info/28/)
- **2024 origin (for the timeline/comparison):** first quota was **¥2,000 +

- **Fuji summit elevation:** **3,776 m (12,388 ft)**. — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/climbing-mount-fuji)
- **Sacred-volcano frame:** UNESCO World Heritage since **2013**, listed for

- **¥4,000 ≈ US$25** (also reported ≈ $13 for the old ¥2,000). — [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/living/pacific_travel/2026-05-01/mount-fuji-yoshida-yamanashi-japan-tourism-21543181.html)
