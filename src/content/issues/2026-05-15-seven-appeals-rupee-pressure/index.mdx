  - kind: prose
    eyebrow: "WHAT THE APPEALS ACTUALLY MOVE"
    title: "Ranked by *money*, not symbolism."
    intro: "Rank the seven appeals by the dollar value they address and the fiscal picture changes. Three of them point at problems large enough to matter to the current account in weeks. The rest are directionally correct and arithmetically marginal."
    data:
      paragraphs:
        - "India's crude import bill runs $137 billion a year — 87% of what the country burns. Of that, 46% transits through or near the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint that closed on March 4. When the government asks citizens to use public transport and work from home, it is addressing the demand side of a $137 billion import at the household margin. The arithmetic does not close."
        - "Gold is the appeal the government converted into policy fastest. At $71.98 billion in FY 2025-26, imports had already grown 24% year-on-year before the war began — and India holds an estimated 25,000 tonnes of household gold (World Gold Council, figure not independently verified from primary source), which means the demand is not a temporary preference. The Sovereign Gold Bond scheme, launched in 2015 to redirect that demand into paper instruments, has not closed the appetite for physical metal. The May 13 duty hike works differently: it taxes the import rather than offering an alternative, which is faster to execute and blunter in effect."
        - "Foreign travel outflows run $16–17 billion a year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme — the largest single category within total LRS outflows of $28.5 billion in FY 2025-26. The government had already addressed this at price: a 20% tax collected at source on LRS remittances above Rs 7 lakh. The May 10 appeal extends the same logic by a different instrument, asking citizens to defer what the earlier measure merely made more expensive."
        - "The cooking oil and fertiliser appeals address real import dependencies, though at absolute values well below crude or gold. Swadeshi over imported branded goods is the softest lever of the seven: the case for import substitution is directionally sound, and it compounds over a decade, but in a crisis measured in weeks, it carries no near-term forex arithmetic."
