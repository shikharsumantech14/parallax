Parallax Lens · internal · August 2026

The revamp, specified

What the website and reader app are now built from — read off the two working prototypes rather than proposed on top of them.

Source of truth: Parallax Brand.dc.html (the mark and its assets), Parallax Web.dc.html (six pages, desktop and mobile web) and Parallax App.dc.html (eleven screens, 393 × 852). Where the two differ, this document says so.

1 · What changed

Three decisions carry the revamp. Everything below is downstream of them.

01 One typeface, seven roles. The old stack ran Zodiak, Erode, DM Mono and a per-topic display face. It is now Literata alone, with weight, size and tracking doing the work the extra families used to do. Anek Devanagari survives for one job only: the bilingual wordmark in the app. 02 The six worlds are data, not themes. Ground, ink, accent, accent-deep, rule and on-accent travel together as one record per world. Every surface reads them; nothing hard-codes a colour. 03 Every issue carries its own instrument. Each of the six has a data component built from its own published figures — a hemicycle, a climate strip, a bar rank, an elevation profile, or a readout of tiles.

2 · The mark

Four concentric circles, one dial position and one letter, on a 300-unit box centred at 150,150. Ring r = 118, stroke 7, ink. Light r = 110, accent. Occluder r = 113 — three units larger than the light, which is what makes the crescent swell at the belly and taper to points — filled in the surface's ground colour. Letter: Literata 700 at 160 units, anchored middle at x = 150, baseline y = 207, so the cap centre lands optically on 150 rather than box-centred. Clear space is 15 units on all four sides.

The occluder sits 15 units from centre, pushed away from the desk's angle θ, so the crescent appears at θ. That angle is the only value that changes between desks — six stations, 60° apart, politics anchoring the dial at 325°.

Politics · 325°Space · 25°Earth · 85°Tech · 145°Travel · 205°Sports · 265°
Outline cutThe everyday mark — masthead, footer, lockups, share cards. Ring stroke thickens as it shrinks: 7 units at 96px, 10 at 40px, 14 at 24px. Reversed cutSolid ink disc, letter knocked out, crescent dropped. This is the seal, and it is also the declared tier below 24px — favicon, app icon, inline end mark. Not a degradation; a different drawing. Cover cutA second crescent outside the ring on the opposite flank, from the same dial. Two accent elements, so it is reserved for covers, posters and the colophon. Never a favicon, avatar or masthead. NeverNo gradient or sphere shading, no off-station dial, no rotation, no second accent colour, no substitute letter, no drop shadow. Assets38 SVGs in brand/: mark, seal and cover per desk, plus favicon, app icon, avatar, watermark, end mark, three lockups and two banners. All generated from the same construction — regenerate rather than hand-edit.

Full construction, misuse plate and banner specimens live in Parallax Brand.dc.html. The six rounds of exploration behind the mark are published to readers on the website's About page.

3 · Colour

One accent per surface, plus ink and paper. The vivid accent is for hairlines, dots, fills and dark grounds; accent-deep is the type-safe sibling on light paper. Never three colours on a page.

Derived values. Secondary ink is the world's ink at 74% on dark grounds, 78% on light; muted meta at 60% and 72%. Tint fills are the accent at 8% (light) or 12% (dark). Nothing else is invented — no second accent, no grey ramp.

On-accent is the text colour that sits on an accent fill: paper white for politics, earth and travel; the world's own near-black for space, tech and sports.

The italic accent word. Every headline carries exactly one, set 400 italic in the world accent while the rest stays upright: The Trojan horse in Parliament. It is stored as three fields — before, accent, after — never as markup inside a string.

Reader text size (app preferences) scales prose only: S 15px, M 16px, L 18.5px. Headlines, meta and stats do not move.

Hairline1px on the world's rule colour. Every list row, card edge, table divider and meta cell. Section rule2px on ink. Used once per screen — above the reading toolbar. Page rule4px on ink at the top of the masthead and the top of the footer. Accent strip4px on accent, one edge only: the primer block and the followed-world row. PaddingWeb 46/40px desktop, 24/20px phone. App 20px horizontal throughout; 6–18px vertical by block. Radius0 everywhere. The only rounded shapes in the system are topic dots and the mock device bezel. ShadowNone on any surface. Elevation is a hairline, not a blur.

7 · Imagery

Two treatments, both scroll-linked, both live in the web and app prototypes. Neither displaces a photograph behind its frame: parallax of that kind is forbidden by name, because the brand promise is a shift of viewpoint, not a sliding backdrop. Photography is warm-toned for politics, earth and travel; moonlit for space; near-black with a lime cast for tech; pitch green for sports. Colour is preferred and is the default for every editorial plate — it is what carries the world palette into the picture. Black and white is permitted where the frame earns it: a portrait, an archival image, or a subject whose colour adds nothing. It is a choice made per picture, never a house style, and a b&w plate still sits in the same hairline frame with the same caption. No grain filters, no press stock.

The plateThe default, one per issue. Sits at prose width inside a 1px frame with 6–8px of inset padding, captioned Plate 02 · the chamber, empty on the left and the source on the right. Fades from 0 to 1 with a 20–22px rise as it reaches the lower third of the viewport — once, on first entry, never on the way back up. The lensConditional. Two viewpoints of one subject in a single frame; scroll drives the wipe edge across, trailed by a 2px accent line. A caption block in ink names the pair (1996 ⇄ 2019 · same ridge). Only where a genuine pair exists — before and after, empty and full, claim and record. Currently travel and politics; the other four run the plate alone. PlacementAfter the opening prose, before the data card. The picture establishes the scene; the graphic then argues about it. SizingWeb 300px plate, 320px lens at the 720px prose column. App 186px plate, 210px lens at 20px margins. Never bleeds past the reading column.

Two further treatments were explored and held back: an aperture (a full-bleed frame that opens from a letterbox band) and an overlay (the issue's figure drawn onto the photograph in accent). Both work, both are in turn 8 of the revamp canvas, and neither is in the shipping system.

8 · Motion and states

Single-property, short, and never decorative. No parallax — the brand promise is a shift of perspective, not of background layers.

Screen enterOpacity 0 → 1 with a 10–14px rise, 280–320ms ease-out. Once per entry, never on scroll. World changeBackground and colour cross-fade over 340ms. The ground changing is the navigation feedback. Data changeBar widths 140ms, seat fills 420ms with a 5ms-per-index stagger, both on cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1). FiguresPlates reveal once at 320ms ease-out. The lens wipe is scroll-linked, not timed — the reader owns the edge. Live dotOpacity 1 → .35 → 1 over 2s, infinite. The only looping animation in the system. HoverBorder colour rule → ink, or muted → ink on text. No lift, no scale, no shadow. SelectedInk fill with paper text (segmented controls, active tab), or accent tint with a 4px accent edge (followed world).

10 · Known gaps

Stated plainly so nobody builds on a floor that isn't there.

01Photography itself. The treatments are specified and built; the pictures are still empty frames. Every slot in both prototypes accepts a dropped image. 02Account recovery and sign-out. Sign in, sign up and link-sent exist; what happens when a link expires does not. 03The archive holds eight issues against a stated twenty-four. The remaining sixteen need real front-matter before launch. 04Every world now carries ten instruments — 60 in all, each with the full contract (subject, how-to-read, labelled axes, legend, live readout, plain-terms line, source). What is still open is editorial: which instrument leads an issue, and whether every one needs a reader-driven control.
