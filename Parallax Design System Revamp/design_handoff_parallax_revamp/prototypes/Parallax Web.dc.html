How Parallax works

Two eyes, one object, a second angle.

Parallax is the shift you get when you move your viewpoint. We do it to one story at a time — the object stays put, the angle changes, and the depth appears.

The mark

A star's distance is measured by looking at it twice, six months apart, from opposite ends of Earth's orbit. Depth is what patience and displacement buy you.

So the mark is a disc lit from one side. The crescent is the part that was hidden until something moved, and it sits at a different hour for each of the six desks — 60° apart, one full turn of the dial across the publication.
