Parallax · component library · v3

Instruments that fit the column and explain themselves.

A reader opens a component because the prose was hard. If the component is harder, it has failed — so every one below carries labelled axes with units, a legend, a stated reading, and a one-line instruction for how to read it. And because an issue is a 720px column of prose, each component is built at a declared size that fits that column rather than the browser window.

Each desk below is shown as a real issue page — the actual prose column, at the actual measure, with its components in the flow at true size. Sixty instruments, ten per desk, no two of them the same shape: every one is built for a different shape of data, from a single distribution to a hierarchy, a network, or a part-of-whole that has to add to a hundred.

— 01 · the size system

Four widths, and a rule for each.

The prose column is 720px and never moves. Everything else is measured against it, so a component's size is a decision about how much it is allowed to interrupt reading — not about how much data will fit.

300 px

S · margin figure

Sits in the margin rail beside the prose on desktop, and in the flow on phone. One number, one shape, no controls. The reader does not stop reading.

720 px

M · column

Exactly the measure of the prose. The default, and the only size that may carry a control. Two or three per issue.

1080 px
breaks out 180 each side

L · breakout

Breaks the column symmetrically. Only when the data genuinely has a long axis — a ranked ladder, a timeline. One per issue at most.

Full bleed

XL · full bleed

Edge to edge, its own section, prose stops. Reserved for the one instrument an issue is built around — never more than one, often none.

PhoneEvery size collapses to the phone measure — 353px inside 20px margins. S stays as drawn; M and L reflow to a single column with the control stack below the graphic; XL becomes M. NeverNo component is wider than its declared size, and none scales with the viewport. A chart that reflows on every window drag cannot be captioned, cited, or screenshotted consistently. BudgetAn issue runs one S per two screens, at most three M, at most one L, and either an XL or an L — not both.

— 02 · the explainability contract

Seven parts. All seven, every time.

A reader arrives at a component having failed to follow the prose. Anything that assumes prior fluency — an unlabelled axis, a colour that stands for something explained elsewhere, a number with no unit — sends them away worse off. These seven are not style; a component missing any of them does not ship.

The Trojan horse in Parliament

India's Parliament has had the same 543 seats since 1976. The freeze was meant to last twenty-five years; it has lasted fifty. In that time the population those seats represent has grown by more than 600 million, and not evenly.

The states that slowed their growth fastest now hold the fewest people per seat. Unfreezing the count rewards the states that grew — an arithmetic no party has been willing to say aloud, because saying it aloud makes the loser obvious.

The asteroid we talked down

By 18 February 2025, an object called 2024 YR4 held a 3.1% chance of striking Earth in December 2032 — the highest impact probability ever recorded for a rock of its size. Six days later it was 0.004%, and nothing about the asteroid had changed.

M · column · fig. 01 viewed along the orbit · width in km

Why the odds went up while nothing moved

How to read this — the shaded band is the uncertainty region: the strip of space the asteroid could pass through on 22 December 2032, given everything known so far. It is not the rock — it is our ignorance, drawn to scale. Earth is the circle. The probability of impact is simply how much of the band Earth covers. Step through the dates: as observations accumulate the band narrows, so Earth's share of it grows — until the band moves off Earth entirely.

The Pacific that no longer resets

Every La Niña is supposed to be the reset — the cool half of a swing that returns the ocean to baseline. Warm phase, cool phase, back where it started. That is what an oscillation means, and it is no longer what the record shows.
