Job assignments that held up on real issues, by kind rather than by topic.

- **`you-think` → INVESTIGATION.** The card *is* the anomaly-first move ("here
  is what you assume, here is the figure that breaks it"). CONVERSATIONAL is
  the obvious second guess and reads flatter.
- **The hero graphic (`climate-strip`, any long series) → AWE**, with an
  INVESTIGATION device in the intro ("Look at X, not Y"). The scale is the
  argument; the intro does the noticing.
- **`jargon-buster`, `three-steps`, `number-sense` → CONVERSATIONAL.** These
  three plain-language kinds make the "CONVERSATIONAL carries at least half"
  floor easy — an issue carrying two or more of them almost never fails it.
- **A tile grid read against itself (`data-readout` comparing two like cases)
  → FORENSIC.** A tile grid of a forecast → CONVERSATIONAL.
- **The closing `prose` → CALM-STRUCTURAL**, with the issue's one image landing
  in its last sentence. That is not a LYRICAL paragraph and should not be
  counted as one — keep the LYRICAL budget unspent unless a whole paragraph is
  doing nothing but landing.

**Why:** the contract's §8 tree answers by rhetorical work, but the kinds
repeat across issues and the same answer keeps coming out; starting from the
kind saves a pass.

**How to apply:** draft the table from this list first, then check it against
§7's hard limits (≥ half CONVERSATIONAL, 3–5 jobs, ≤ 1 SATIRICAL, ≤ 1 LYRICAL)
and only re-argue the rows that break one. See [[rewrite-constraints]].
