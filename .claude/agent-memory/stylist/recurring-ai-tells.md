The contract's §6 catalog is the law; this is which of the seventeen keep
showing up in *this* publication's drafts, plus two near-misses the catalog
does not name.

- **Tell 4, abstract-noun label, always lands in the `paradox` intro.** The
  drafter names the rhetorical shape instead of stating the claim: "there is
  an honest tension in that", and in the pre-rewrite version "That is the
  point." Fix: delete the label, keep the claim, join with *and yet*.
- **Tell 6, stacked reframes, lands where a component is already the
  reversal.** `you-think` is structurally "it is not X, it is Y" — two panels.
  An intro above it that also runs a "not X" reversal spends the issue's one
  allowance twice. Count the component, not just the prose.
- **A restatement written into `skimCaption` is invisible.** `skimCaption`
  renders only in Skim mode. Contract §5 says so since 2026-09-13, but drafts
  keep putting the reader's-words restatement there. The sentence the reader
  meets after a graphic is the **next section's intro**. Check every seam.
- **Near-miss, not in the catalog: the analogy that contradicts itself.** "You
  buy a time, not a place" illustrated by "a train berth" — a berth *is* a
  place. A reader panel catches these; the tell audit does not. Read every
  analogy back against the sentence it is illustrating.
- **Near-miss: the eyebrow that outlives the fix.** Eyebrows are outside the
  stylist's remit, so a corrected intro can sit under an eyebrow still stating
  the thing that was corrected ("THREE MOVES" over an intro explaining that two
  of the three are not moves). Always flag the eyebrow when the intro under it
  was fixed for that reason.

**Why:** these five account for most of what a second reader-panel pass would
otherwise send back, and three of them are invisible to a field-by-field read.

**How to apply:** run them as a checklist over the `paradox` intro, every
`you-think` section, every seam between a graphic and the next intro, and every
analogy, before the general §6 sweep.
