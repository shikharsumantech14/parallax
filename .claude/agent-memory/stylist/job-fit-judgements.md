# Job-fit judgements that held up

**The "CONVERSATIONAL EXPLAINER carries at least half the sections" rule is
arithmetic, and storyboards get it wrong.**

**Why:** the composer sketches jobs in prose ("INVESTIGATION opens, CONVERSATIONAL
carries the middle, CALM-STRUCTURAL closes") and does not count. On the
delimitation rewrite (9 sections) the storyboard's own split was 2 / 4 / 3 and
claimed 4 of 9 was "over half the sections, as required". It is not.

**How to apply:** count before you accept a storyboard's job sketch. When the
count is short, the section to re-read is usually the one whose *prose* explains
a mechanism while its *slot* (a vote, a result, a closer) says CALM-STRUCTURAL.
A `vote-result` intro that glosses two terms of art in two sentences
("Changing the Constitution needs two-thirds of those present and voting. In a
division vote every MP's yes or no is counted") is a walk-through — the
decision tree (§8) sends it to CONVERSATIONAL, not to the closer's default.
The contract's hard blending rule outranks the storyboard's sketch; report the
reallocation as storyboard drift with the reason.

## Reliable slot → job fits so far

- `number-sense` / `data-readout` as section 1, no intro → **INVESTIGATION**
  ("look at this number"). The numeral and the eyebrow carry the opening move;
  an intro here also eats the 80-word words-before-first-graphic budget.
- `timeline` whose intro glosses the issue's one term of art →
  **INVESTIGATION**. The dates *are* the anomaly.
- `you-think`, `bill-breakdown`, `analogy`, the hero chart → **CONVERSATIONAL**.
  These four are the reliable half.
- `you-think` as section 1 **with no `intro`** → **INVESTIGATION**. The belief
  panel is the anomaly held up first, and the missing intro is what keeps the
  words-before-first-graphic count inside 80. Same slot logic as `number-sense`
  at 1.
- `data-readout` → **AWE** only when one tile is a *zero* or another flat
  landing fact, and then the awe lives in the tile, never in the intro. The
  intro stays a plain hand-off from the previous section, and that is not a job
  mismatch: it is AWE's own calibration (the awe is in the fact). If every tile
  is a rate or a count, the section is CONVERSATIONAL.
- `paradox`, `quote` → **CALM-STRUCTURAL**, always. Both are "the official line
  beside the physical fact with the sentence that joins them".
- `number-sense` mid-issue (the ₹-conversion card) → **AWE**. Its whole shape is
  the AWE cadence — a flat fact, then the conversion to a scale the reader owns.
  It is the one AWE section a non-space issue can carry honestly. **Exception:
  the politics desk.** Where the number is a following, a turnout or a body
  count, AWE reads as the writer being impressed by the subject, and restraint
  is the safer register (§8's tie-break). It goes CONVERSATIONAL there — which
  is also what keeps the ≥ half count reachable on a nine-section issue, where
  the arithmetic is 5, not 4.
- `three-steps` → **CONVERSATIONAL** by the decision tree's own words ("a
  walk-through, one step at a time"). Do not upgrade it to FORENSIC because the
  mechanism is technical.
- `tactics-pitch`, `match-stat-line` → **FORENSIC**: a mechanism and the match
  that tested it. Two FORENSIC sections in a row is fine when they are the
  mechanism and its proof.

- `descent-profile` (or any hero trajectory chart) as section 1 with no
  `intro` → **INVESTIGATION**. Third confirmation of the same rule: a
  first-slot graphic with no intro is always INVESTIGATION, whatever the kind.
  The graphic is the anomaly held up, and the missing intro is what keeps the
  80-word budget.
- `orbit-trace` / any two-things-in-one-space closer → **LYRICAL
  COMPRESSION**, and it is the one paragraph an issue gets. The landing line
  is the last clause of the `intro`, after the numbers are laid out plainly —
  lyrical cadence *before* the figures reads as ornament.
- `timeline` whose intro hands the reader a new fact about *why* the dates
  are what they are → **CONVERSATIONAL**, not INVESTIGATION. The two
  `timeline` fits differ by what the intro does: glossing a term of art while
  the dates speak is INVESTIGATION; explaining where the dates came from is a
  walk-through. On the ISS issue this reallocation was also what got the
  CONVERSATIONAL count from 3 of 8 to 4 of 8, i.e. to exactly half.

- `benchmark-chart` as section 1 with no `intro` → **INVESTIGATION**. Fourth
  confirmation; the annotation on the highlighted bar does the "look at this".
- `you-think` **not** at section 1, with an intro that restates the previous
  graphic and then names the reader's own assumption → **CONVERSATIONAL**, even
  though the belief panel is an anomaly. The restatement is the tell: it is the
  CONVERSATIONAL card's own opening move (restate → the thing you assumed →
  the correction). Only the intro-less first slot makes `you-think`
  INVESTIGATION.
- `core-sample` as the hero, with a researcher quote inside the intro →
  **FORENSIC**. Two short sentences of mechanism plus the human stake is the
  card exactly; `power-flow` next to it stays CONVERSATIONAL because its intro
  is doing arithmetic-glossing, not stakes.

**The intro's opening move decides the job, not the kind.** A
`benchmark-chart` is an anomaly-as-graphic (INVESTIGATION) *unless* its intro
opens on an everyday-object analogy — the cricket field setting on the Arsenal
issue — in which case it is CONVERSATIONAL and the INVESTIGATION slot belongs
to the hero graphic instead.

## The arithmetic outranks the intro-less-first-slot rule

The "`you-think` at slot 1 with no `intro` → INVESTIGATION" fit is real but it
is not the top of the order. On the eleven-bills issue (9 sections, so the floor
is 5 CONVERSATIONAL) applying it left only 4, and nothing else in the issue
could be re-read into a walk-through without misdescribing it: `bill-passage` is
a mechanism with stakes, `quote` and `timeline` are the closer pair.

**The resolution is to move INVESTIGATION onto the hero, not to force a job
elsewhere.** A `bill-funnel` whose whole point is negative space — four bars
that do not shorten until the one that costs time — is the anomaly-as-graphic in
its purest form, and its intro glosses "committee" precisely so the reader can
*see* the gap. Section 1 then takes CONVERSATIONAL, which its own card
(assumption → the number that breaks it) already fits.

**How to apply:** when the half-rule is short by one on a nine-section issue,
look at the hero before you look at the closers. The hero is the section most
likely to be carrying INVESTIGATION already and labelled something else.

## `number-sense` late in the issue, against a second scale → AWE

The earlier note ("`number-sense` mid-issue → AWE") held again on the
NRI-arrivals issue, and the shape is now clear enough to state as a rule: a
`number-sense` whose headline figure is a **ratio between the issue's subject
and something much larger** (468 domestic visits per foreign tourist) is doing
scale, not explanation. Its intro's job is to hand the reader the second scale
and gloss it ("domestic tourist visits, which means trips Indians make inside
India"), then get out of the way. Do not reach for CONVERSATIONAL just because
the intro carries a gloss — every job glosses now, under Rule 0.

## A nine-section travel issue that landed 5 / 2 / 1 / 1

INVESTIGATION → CONVERSATIONAL → INVESTIGATION → CONVERSATIONAL →
CONVERSATIONAL → CONVERSATIONAL → CONVERSATIONAL → AWE → CALM-STRUCTURAL.
Five CONVERSATIONAL of nine clears the half-rule with nothing to spare, and the
two INVESTIGATIONs are the hero (`benchmark-chart` with a reference line that is
not a country) and the `timeline` (the year the halves swapped). The `quote`
closer is CALM-STRUCTURAL, as always. Worth keeping as the template for a
"the official number is two things added together" issue.

## `voice:` (the schema's top-level field)

Dominant job by section count. In practice this is **CONVERSATIONAL EXPLAINER**
for any conforming issue, because the contract mandates it carry half. Set it
anyway — the fact grid drops to three cells when it is absent. Position: after
`readTimeMinutes`, before `primer`. The backlist runs both quoted and unquoted;
quote it, since the value contains a space.
