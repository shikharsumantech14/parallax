# When the unglossed term is not in a field you write

Contract rule 2 says gloss on first use. On a statute-dense issue the terms of
art (*lapsed, assent, the gaps, NCTP*) sit in `label`s, `note`s and inside
verbatim quotations, all of which Step 5 protects. Three moves, cheapest first.

1. **Replace the term in its own label rather than buying a note.** "It then
   lapsed, still pending in the Rajya Sabha" → "Still pending in the Rajya
   Sabha, it died when that Lok Sabha ended." The gloss costs four words and no
   new field; a note that explains *lapsed* costs ten and adds a block to a
   section the CHROME-HEAVY heuristic already flags. A term used once anywhere
   in the issue does not need to be taught, only avoided.
2. **A bare noun in a label gets its owner, not a definition.** "a committee on
   the gaps" → "a committee on gaps in the 2019 Act": the reader met the 2019
   Act two events earlier, so attaching the abstraction to a thing already on
   the page is three words and closes it. Same move for an unnamed object in
   the head ("a district magistrate issues" → "issues the certificate").
3. **An acronym inside a locked quotation is expanded upstream, in the framing
   sentence.** "…representatives of the NCTP…" is verbatim and untouchable; the
   sentence two before it already names the body, so the repair is to put the
   acronym in apposition there ("the National Council of Transgender Persons,
   the NCTP, a government advisory body"). Two words, no new name — an
   abbreviation of an entity already named is not a twelfth name, though the
   gate's capitalisation heuristic will count it.

## Two labels measuring different things over one graphic

An `eyebrow` carrying a number and a `title` naming a span will be read as one
unit, and if they measure different intervals every numerate reader does the
other arithmetic and has to be corrected by the caption. Both fields are
flag-only, so this needs the launching agent's instruction — and when it comes,
**edit the title, not the eyebrow.** The eyebrow holds the number the editor
notes chose deliberately (usually the interval whose endpoints are both
anchored), eyebrows on an issue run to a two-or-three-word house pattern that a
self-labelling fix breaks, and the storyboard's title cap is nearly always the
one with room. The stack may still show stages past the title's span; a title
states the finding, it does not enumerate the cards.
