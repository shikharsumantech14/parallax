# Say the two units do not convert, out loud, in the intro

**When an issue measures one quantity in two units and the storyboard forbids
converting between them, the reader's arithmetic stop is not fixed by a gloss.
It is fixed by telling the reader there is no arithmetic to do.**

The tech issue measured the open-closed model gap in *months* (a time-to-catch-up
figure) and in *index points* (a score difference). The dossier's own note said
8 points ÷ the index's per-month slope is 6.9 months, not four, so the
storyboard ruled: never convert one into the other, present them side by side.
The drafter honoured the ruling by simply not mentioning it — and the analytical
persona stopped dead, tried to reconcile the chart's four sampled bars against
the section title's "eight points", failed, and then *doubted the four-month
figure from three sections earlier*. An unstated non-relationship reads as a
mistake.

**The shape that worked**, at 16 extra words in the `intro`:

> "Eight points ahead on the index, four months ahead in time. Those are two
> readings of the same gap, and neither one converts into the other."

Both units in the first sentence, in parallel. The second sentence names the
relationship (same gap) and closes the door (no conversion). Nothing is
converted, so the storyboard ruling holds; nothing is left implicit, so the
arithmetic never starts.

## The second half of that fix goes in `plain`, not the intro

The same section drew four bars (58, 33, 25, 3) chosen as the extremes, which
do not average to eight. That is a **drawing choice**, so it belongs below the
graphic, in `plain`, with no numerals: *"These four are the widest and the
narrowest, not an average."* Putting it in the intro pushed the intro to the
45-word cap and buried the units bridge, which is the part the reader needs
*before* seeing the chart. Two fixes, two fields, in render order.

## The generalisation

Any issue carrying a headline figure in one unit and a graphic in another needs
one sentence saying which of these three it is:
- the same number in different units (then convert, once, and show the working),
- two readings of one thing that do not convert (then say so),
- two different things (then the eyebrow or title is wrong, and that is a flag).

The drafter will almost never write this sentence, because the writer holds both
readings at once and cannot see the seam. The panel always finds it.
