The lexicon is the allowlist; this is what the operator has *ruled* and what
the desks have actually carried in practice.

**Kept by ruling**

- **`ka` in a hook, standing beside a figure** — "Everest ka ticket: $15,000".
  Ruled binding 2026-09-13 (queue storyboard §9.2). It also forced the
  contract's precision test to be re-worded: Hindi never modifies, hedges or
  counts a figure, but a possessive *standing beside* one is allowed. Do not
  re-open this, and do not generalise it into Hindi attaching to numbers.
- **A plain-English dek is an acceptable answer to rule 12.** Rule 12 says the
  dek carries the Hindi when the title has none; the operator confirmed L1 when
  no candidate passed all four tests inside a short dek. "When in doubt, leave
  it out" outranks the dek slot.

**Observed density, once styled**

- **Travel ran at effectively zero.** One styled travel issue carried exactly
  two Hindi words, both in the signed hook, and the reader panel confirmed no
  Hindi word was load-bearing anywhere — the Tamil-first persona lost nothing.
  The lexicon rates travel "medium"; the register still reads correct at L1.
  Treat the per-desk density column as a ceiling, never a quota.
- **The Indian ground can be carried entirely by currency, scale and habit**
  when a dossier holds no Indian fact: ₹ beside every $ and ¥, and an everyday
  Indian booking habit (a temple's timed entry) used as an analogy. An analogy
  is form, needs no source, and adds no name to the ≤ 12 count.

**Why:** two of the four Hindi tests are judgement calls, and the operator's ear
outranks the lexicon; recording the rulings stops each run from re-litigating
them.

**How to apply:** before adding any Hindi word, check it here first, then the
lexicon. Before flagging "no Indian ground", check whether currency plus a habit
analogy already carries it. See [[job-fit-judgements]].
