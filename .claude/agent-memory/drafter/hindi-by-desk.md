**Politics: the honest answer is usually zero.** On the delimitation rewrite
(2026-09-13) every candidate failed at least one test and the finished issue
carries exactly one Hindi phrase — *"Credit nahi chahiye"*, and only because
it is a **verbatim quoted phrase serving as a section title**, sourced, set
roman.
**Why:** the desk rule is "connective words only, no Hindi in or near a joke,
restraint is the register", and a politics issue is wall-to-wall numbers,
statutes and institutions — which is exactly where the precision test bars
Hindi.
**How to apply:** on politics, draft L1 throughout and let a quoted phrase be
the only exception. Do not go looking for a place to put one.

**`hisaab` failed on politics and would pass on sports.** The lexicon marks it
"eyebrow-grade: THE HISAAB", which reads as a green light — but the eyebrow
that wanted it sat directly above a `number-sense` carrying 30 lakh vs 18
lakh, and the desk row says politics carries the fewest words of any desk and
connectives only. `THE GAP` did the same job at L1.
**How to apply:** the per-desk row in the lexicon outranks a word's own "use"
column. A noun that is eyebrow-grade on sports is not eyebrow-grade on
politics.

**A Hindi phrase inside a section `title` is safe from the blocking check but
not from the contract.** `title` is not a precision field, so no HINDI-FIELD
flag fires; the four tests are still yours to apply, and the test that
matters is the skip test — Karthik must lose nothing. Carry the meaning in
the section's `intro`, the quote and the `followup` in English, and the title
becomes decoration rather than load.

Earth's awe nouns (*jhoola*, *samundar*) and the worked example in the
contract's §9 are a different register entirely — do not import that comfort
level onto politics. See [[word-budgets-that-bite]].
