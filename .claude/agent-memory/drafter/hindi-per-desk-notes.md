Observations from authoring, not restatements of the contract or lexicon.

**`ka` is operator-signed but not in the lexicon.** `_voice-core.md` §9's
signed hook is *"Everest ka ticket: $15,000… Fuji ka ticket: ₹2,300…"*, and
§2's precision test was re-worded on 2026-09-13 to allow a possessive beside a
figure. But `ka` is in neither table of `hinglish-lexicon.md`, so
`check:prose` cannot see it: it is not in `HINDI_ALLOWED` (no
HINDI-CONSECUTIVE / HINDI-LOAD-BEARING flag) and not in the banned set (no
HINDI-BANNED flag). The gate is silent on it either way. Flag it for the
operator as a lexicon gap rather than assuming the silence is approval.

**The gate only knows the lexicon's words.** A Hindi or Indian-English word
absent from both tables passes unremarked. That is not a licence — the
verifier and the reader panel still read it. It does mean the burden is on the
writer, not the tool.

**Words that felt natural but were dropped on the travel desk:** *darshan*,
*tatkal*. Both are proper-noun-ish scheme/ritual names rather than register
words, neither is in the lexicon, and both cost a name against the ceiling if
you anchor them to an institution (Tirupati, IRCTC). "a temple booking, or a
train berth reserved weeks ahead" carries the same Indian ground, needs no
name, and passes Karthik (P4, no Hindi) outright.

**An issue with Hindi only in the hook still reads placed**, provided the
Indian ground is carried by ₹/lakh, an Indian habit the reader owns, and the
Indian-English sense of the everyday words. On the travel desk "line" is
already doing that work — the lexicon's per-desk row lists it, and an issue
about queues can lean on it throughout without a single Hindi word elsewhere.
When the storyboard asks for no Hindi beyond the hook, that is usually right.
